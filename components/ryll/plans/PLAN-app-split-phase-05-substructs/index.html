    Phase 5: Panel state sub-structs¶

    Phase 5 of PLAN-app-split.md.

    Planning effort¶

    High. Phase 4 was a move: the compiler proved it, and the only judgement was where each block went. This phase changes who owns state, which means picking a boundary per panel and living with it. Two of the master plan's premises for the phase turned out to be wrong — the two-pass workarounds do not go away (finding 1), and the two panels it names as the self-contained starting point are the least self-contained of the three (finding 3). Deciding what the phase promises instead, and in what order, is the planning work.

    Review effort: high. The diff will read as mechanical and mostly is, but three of its decisions are cheap to wave through and expensive to reverse once three panels share the shape.

    Scope¶

    In scope — three panel state clusters move out of RyllApp into structs owned by their app/ui/*.rs modules, each with a show method taking &mut self plus the shared state it needs as explicit arguments:

    • TrafficViewer — 11 fields, mod.rs:344-354.
    • WebdavPanel — 11 fields, mod.rs:331-341.
    • UsbPanel — 15 fields, mod.rs:248-255, :320-323 and :326-328.

    That is 37 of RyllApp's fields. With them: a named pub(in crate::app) method on each panel for every state transition that today happens as a field write from events.rs or connection.rs (41 of them — finding 2), the is_open() accessor observe_ui_state needs, the UsbPanelOutcome type that replaces two &mut out-params, and the documentation the move makes stale.

    Out of scope, each with its reason:

    • The notifications panel. Its panel-local state is two fields (show_notifications_panel, notifications_panel_was_open_last_frame). notifications and notification_snapshots are shared with every channel and with the bug-report path, so they cannot move. A struct wrapping two bools costs more in indirection than it removes. Decision 1.
    • Region select. Its three fields are written by trigger_snapshot.rs:326 and :344 and read by bug_report.rs:576 — it is bug-report state that happens to draw an overlay, not panel state. Decision 1.
    • The stats bar, surface, cursor and keys clusters. Session state that the bug-report snapshot reads, not panel state. Moving them into app/ui/ would put the authority for stats, bandwidth and held_keys inside draw code. Out, and not merely deferred: this phase takes the position that they belong on RyllApp.
    • Removing the two-pass workarounds. Finding 1. The phase keeps the deferral and changes where it lives.
    • reconnect.rs at 881 lines. Finding 5. Phase 4 handed it forward and phase 7's section already requires checking app/ for oversize files, so it has a home.
    • The paste-error dialog's take() tidy. Finding 1, site 4. Three lines in a file this phase otherwise does not touch. Future work.

    What the survey found¶

    The master plan's phase 5 section was written on 2026-10-10, before phases 2, 3 and 4 executed and before ui_state.rs arrived from an unrelated plan. Five premises were checked: two hold, one is incomplete, and two are wrong. Each correction has been made at source in the master plan's phase 5 section and in the docs/plans/index.md row, so this plan explains them rather than being the only record of them.

    Finding 1 — sub-structs do not remove the two-pass workarounds¶

    The master plan says to "remove the two-pass render-then-act workarounds where disjoint borrows make them unnecessary". There are five such sites. Not one is caused by RyllApp being a god struct, so not one is removed by splitting it up:

    Site Why it defers Sub-struct effect
    ui/usb_panel.rs:35 (usb_action) Panel::show's closure holds &mut self; executing needs usb_tx and writes two error fields Becomes local. Under decision 2 every field it touches is in UsbPanel, so the out-param goes; the deferral stays, inside one module
    ui/usb_panel.rs:55 (open_usb_bug_report) Targets the bug dialog's state via open_bug_dialog Becomes explicit. UsbPanel cannot reach the dialog, so it returns UsbPanelOutcome — the shape draw_surface already uses
    mod.rs:1124 (outcome.open_channel_bug_report) Same cross-panel shape, already a returned outcome Unchanged; it is the pattern being generalised
    ui/dialogs.rs:129 (paste error) if let Some(ref msg) = self.paste_error_message holds &self across the closure that wants to clear it None. The same borrow of the same field inside impl DialogsState. Fixed by take(), not by sub-structing
    ui/region_select.rs:19 ctx.input(|i| …) holds egui's input lock while the completion needs self.notifications None. Working under egui's lock is a deadlock risk at any struct shape

    So the phase cannot promise the two-pass goes away. What it can promise, and what the definition of done measures, is that no deferred action crosses a module boundary as a &mut out-param: two sites change from an out-param to either a local or a named return type, and the three that remain are deferred for reasons a reviewer can read in a comment.

    Finding 2 — events.rs and connection.rs own most of the state transitions¶

    This is the finding that decides what the phase is worth, and the master plan does not mention it.

    The panels do not drive their own state. Channel events do. self.usb_* and self.webdav_* are written 41 times from outside app/ui/. "Outside app/ui/" is the count's definition and it matters: three further writers live inside app/ui/, in other panels' modules, and finding 3 covers them.

    Writer USB WebDAV
    events.rs — per-event handlers 11 13
    connection.rs:140-158 — reconnect reset 9 8
    Total 20 21

    events.rs already groups them into named handlers — handle_usb_channel_ready (:318), handle_usb_device_connected (:324), handle_usb_device_disconnected (:332), handle_usb_connect_failed (:340), handle_webdav_channel_ready (:348), handle_webdav_sharing_started (:354), handle_webdav_sharing_stopped (:363), handle_webdav_error (:371), plus handle_disconnected (:427) and clear_usb_operation_flags (:604). Each is a handful of field writes with a name already on it.

    That maps one-to-one onto a method per transition on the sub-struct, which is the phase's real payoff and a better one than encapsulation for its own sake: 41 scattered field assignments become a named API per panel, and "what can change the USB panel's state, and from where" becomes a question you answer by reading one file instead of grepping three. It also means the phase is bigger than the master plan's "move its field cluster" implies — moving the fields without moving the transitions would leave events.rs reaching into a sub-struct's private fields, which is a rename, not an encapsulation.

    Finding 3 — the master plan's starting point is inverted¶

    The master plan says to "start with the most self-contained (the WebDAV and USB panels, whose fields are already prefixed)". The prefixes are real. The premise is backwards: by finding 2's count, WebDAV and USB are the least self-contained clusters in app/ui/, with 21 and 20 external writers. The traffic viewer, which the master plan never names, has zero — nothing outside ui/traffic_panel.rs reads or writes any of its eleven fields.

    USB's fields are also in three blocks separated by about 70 lines of unrelated fields, not one cluster. WebDAV's eleven are contiguous.

    So the step order inverts: the traffic viewer goes first, because it is the one with the least outside it and therefore the cheapest place to settle the shape. WebDAV follows as the template for the event API, and USB — three blocks, 20 writers, and the two out-params — goes last.

    Correction, 2026-11-25. This finding first said the traffic viewer had no external writer at all, and that step 5a was "a pure field move the compiler can prove". Both were wrong, and step 5a's sub-agent stopped and said so before changing anything. The error was in the survey's method: its grep excluded the whole of app/ui/, which is where two of the writers live, so the result supported only the narrower claim that nothing outside app/ui/ touches the fields.

    Ten of the traffic viewer's eleven fields are genuinely local to ui/traffic_panel.rs. The eleventh, the open flag, has four users and only one is the panel:

    • ui/stats_bar.rs:359-361 — the hamburger panel menu, one ui.checkbox(&mut self.show_*, …) per panel. This needs a &mut bool, so a read-only accessor is not enough. It drives all three panels' flags, so 5b and 5c are affected too.
    • ui/keys.rs:48 — F11 toggles the traffic viewer. A read and a write.
    • trigger_snapshot.rs:276-278 — the bug-report snapshot reads all three.
    • The panel itself, gating its own draw.

    Decision 3 says how the flag is exposed. No step in this phase is a pure field move.

    Finding 4 — ui_state.rs is a consumer the plan never saw¶

    observe_ui_state (trigger_snapshot.rs:269) reads eight panel-open flags plus reconnect_state each frame and feeds them to UiStateTracker for bug reports. It arrived in e62a4a7 ("Let bug reports show ryll's own interface", #562), months after the master plan's phase 5 section was written.

    Three of its eight flags — show_traffic_viewer (:276), show_usb_panel (:277), show_webdav_panel (:278) — are in this phase's scope. The ten element names in that array are a published contract: they are what bug reports contain and docs/diagnostics.md:241-242 lists them. Decision 3 keeps the names and changes only what is read beside them.

    Finding 5 — reconnect.rs breaches the plan's own 800-line rule¶

    Phase 4's definition of done requires that no file under ryll/src/app/ except mod.rs exceed 800 lines. reconnect.rs is 881. Phase 4 recorded this honestly rather than ticking it: it was 533 at phase 3's merge, and #718 added 350 lines to it on develop itself, in a file phase 4 never touches.

    Worth saying plainly because the usual guard does not catch it: tools/source-size-ratchet.py fires at 1,500 lines, so 881 is invisible to CI and only this plan's own criterion sees it. Out of scope here (it is reconnect logic, not panel state); phase 7's section already requires checking app/ for new oversize files, which is where it lands.

    What was checked and holds¶

    • Every app/ui/*.rs file is an impl RyllApp block whose draw methods take &mut self. Phase 4 was the pure move it claimed to be, so the starting point is exactly what the master plan assumes.
    • eframe::App::ui is 48 lines and constructs no panel or window, so the call sequence this phase edits is the short dispatcher phase 4 promised.
    • RyllApp::new is 303 lines with a 133-field struct literal, and carries #[expect(clippy::too_many_lines)] (mod.rs:467) against a threshold of 200. Moving 37 fields into 3 sub-structs takes the literal to about 99 lines and new to about 269 — still over 200, so the exemption stays fulfilled and the build stays green. The margin is 69 lines; decision 5 handles the reason text, which stops being true well before the number does.
    • RyllApp::new is the only one of the thirteen #[expect(clippy::too_many_lines)] functions in this phase's scope. (c70d276, which closed out phase 6, said "two of the thirteen annotated functions are the ones phase 5 would touch". The other twelve are in bugreport.rs, main.rs, rdp.rs and the renderer and compression crates; none is in app/. Noted because a commit message cannot be corrected.)
    • connection.rs:63 is 154 lines. Blocker note n8 lists it as an offender over "clippy's 100-line threshold" owned by phase 6 — but phase 6 measured the threshold at 200, so it is not an offender and needs nothing. Recorded so the next reader does not go looking for work that does not exist.

    Decisions¶

    1. Three clusters move, two do not. The traffic viewer, WebDAV and USB move; the notifications panel and region select stay. The test applied was whether the cluster's state has a reader outside its own module that is not observe_ui_state: region select has two (trigger_snapshot.rs, bug_report.rs) and they are the drivers of it, and the notifications panel's substantive state is shared by every producer in the process. A sub-struct that half the codebase reaches through is a rename, not an encapsulation.

    Note this is a different test from the one finding 3 applies. USB and WebDAV have 41 external writers and still move, because every one of those writers is a channel event that becomes a named method on the panel (finding 2). Region select's external writers are not transitions that belong to the panel — they are the bug-report flow deciding when a region is being selected.

    2. The sub-struct owns its channel sender and every transition that drives it. UsbPanel holds usb_tx and WebdavPanel holds webdav_tx; the 41 field writes in events.rs and connection.rs become calls to pub(in crate::app) methods on the panel, one per transition, named after the handler that calls it. connection.rs:140-158 becomes two calls — self.usb.reset_for_session(usb_tx) and self.webdav.reset_for_session(webdav_tx) — because that block is one transition (a new session) and not seventeen.

    This is the decision a reviewer is most likely to disagree with, and the objection is sound: the sender's lifetime is the connection's, not the panel's, so this puts two lifetimes in one struct, and a panel struct replaced piecewise on reconnect is a trap for whoever next adds a field to it.

    Taken anyway. The panel is the sender's only consumer, so the alternative — leaving usb_tx on RyllApp and passing &Option<Sender<UsbCommand>> into show — keeps the field on the struct this phase exists to shrink and adds an argument to every call site to do it. And reset_for_session taking the new sender is what makes the trap safe: the one method that installs a sender is also the one method that resets the state, so a field added to the struct is added in front of the code that must clear it. That is strictly better than today, where the reset is seventeen lines in another file.

    3. The open flag stays private behind is_open and set_open, with toggle only where a hotkey flips it, and the element names are pinned by a test. Each moved sub-struct gets is_open(&self) -> bool and set_open(&mut self, bool) as pub(in crate::app). The panel menu reads, offers and writes back —

    let mut open = self.traffic_viewer.is_open();
if ui.checkbox(&mut open, "Traffic").changed() {
    self.traffic_viewer.set_open(open);
}

    — which costs three lines per checkbox and keeps ui.checkbox's &mut bool requirement from forcing either a public field or an open_mut() -> &mut bool getter.

    toggle(&mut self) is added only to a panel that something flips without reading it first, which in this phase is the traffic viewer alone: F11 calls it. WebDAV and USB have no hotkey, so a toggle on either would be an uncalled pub(in crate::app) method — dead code, which make lint rejects. Writing one and silencing the lint would be worse than not writing it.

    Two alternatives were rejected. Making open pub(in crate::app) is the smaller diff, but the first field of the new struct being public is what the next field will cite. Leaving the three flags on RyllApp and moving only the other fields is defensible — three of the flag's four users are outside the panel — but it puts a conditional per panel back into eframe::App::ui, which phase 4 worked to make a flat sequence.

    The array in observe_ui_state keeps one line per element and its ten string literals unchanged; only the right-hand side changes, from self.show_usb_panel to self.usb.is_open(). A test asserts the ten names in order, so a later field rename cannot silently change what a bug report contains. Passing the sub-structs into observe_ui_state instead was rejected because it makes a bug-report function's signature grow with every panel that moves.

    4. UsbPanel::show returns UsbPanelOutcome, not out-params. One field, open_bug_report: bool, mirroring SurfaceOutcome from phase 4. usb_action disappears into the struct under decision 2 and does not appear in the outcome. A named return type rather than a bare bool because the surface's outcome set that precedent and because the next cross-panel action to escape USB should have somewhere to go that is not another parameter.

    5. new's exemption reason is corrected in the same step that breaks it. The reason reads "one struct literal naming every field", which stops being true the moment the first cluster moves. Step 5d rewrites it. The done-criterion is make lint passing, which mechanically proves the #[expect] is still fulfilled; a figure in a comment would not.

    6. One pull request, four commits, with a shape gate after the first panel. The three moves share one pattern; three pull requests would mean three reviews of it, and if the first panel's shape is wrong the other two are wrong the same way and would be redone regardless. So they land together — but the back brief gates on the shape of TrafficViewer being agreed before WebDAV and USB are written, which is the protection splitting the PR would have bought at a quarter of the review. The gate sits after the traffic viewer rather than after WebDAV because the traffic viewer is the pure field move (finding 3): it settles the struct shape without the event API confusing the question.

    Risks and mitigations¶

    1. A transition is moved to a method and quietly changes behaviour. This is the phase's real hazard and it is not one the compiler catches: handle_webdav_sharing_started writes four fields in an order, and a method that writes three of them, or writes them from a slightly different starting state, compiles and is wrong. Mitigation: each moved handler is reviewed as a before/after of the field writes, not as a diff of the call site — the reviewer reads git show develop:ryll/src/app/events.rs for the original body of every handler the step touches. The field-write count per handler is in finding 2's table, so a method that writes a different number of fields is visible without reading both versions.

    2. The draw order changes and nothing notices. egui reserves panel space in draw order and windows are z-ordered; phase 4 preserved both and said so in a comment in ui. Changing self.draw_usb_panel(ui, ctx) to self.usb.show(ui, ctx) is where that gets quietly reordered, and UsbPanelOutcome adds a statement after the call. Mitigation: the reviewer diffs the call sequence in eframe::App::ui against develop line for line, and the operator runs the result against a guest before the pull request — which is the only check in existence for this, as phase 4 recorded.

    3. UsbPanel and WebdavPanel mix connection and panel lifetimes. Decision 2's accepted cost. A future field may need clearing on reconnect and not get it. Mitigation: reset_for_session is the single method that both installs the sender and clears the state, so the reset is in front of anyone adding a field, plus the doc comment steps 5b and 5c require. The reviewer checks that comment exists and is accurate against connection.rs:142-158 as it stands on develop.

    4. A panel accessor is added that should not exist. The compiler forces a new pub(in crate::app) accessor for every outside reader, and the easy fix for a reader that should not be reading panel state is to add one. Mitigation: any accessor beyond is_open, set_open, reset_for_session and the named transitions is argued for in the pull request. The done criteria make the inventory greppable.

    5. The phase is larger than the master plan implies. Finding 2 turns "move a field cluster" into "move a cluster and 41 transitions". That is more diff than the master plan budgeted, and it is the operator's call before the work starts rather than the reviewer's after: back brief point 1.
