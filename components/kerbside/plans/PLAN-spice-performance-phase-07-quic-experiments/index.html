    Phase 7: QUIC client leg, experiments¶

    Master plan: PLAN-spice-performance.md. Planning effort: high. The phase turns on a transport design decision, crosses two repositories, and the master plan's section for it was written on 2026-09-27 without reading the code.

    The master plan's phase 7 section is the brief this plan refines: a time-boxed experiment whose output is a go or no-go and, if go, a phase plan for phase 8. That stands. What follows grounds it in the code as of 2026-11-11 and changes two things the survey found wrong: what the deciding measurement actually decides, and what the rig can measure today.

    Scope¶

    In:

    • a packet-loss term for the shaped-link rig, which it does not have and the phase's headline measurement needs;
    • a transport-only comparison of per-channel TCP against one QUIC connection with a stream per channel, under loss, as a gate before any SPICE integration;
    • past that gate, an end-to-end prototype behind a default-off cargo feature in Kerbside and a matching feature in ryll: one QUIC connection per session, one bidirectional stream per SPICE channel, client leg only;
    • measurements of input-to-echo latency under loss, connect time, behaviour across a path change, and the cost of falling back to TCP;
    • a written verdict, a recommendation on UDP exposure, and the audit-trail consequences recorded;
    • if go, a phase plan for phase 8.

    Out:

    • the backend leg, which stays stock SPICE over TCP. No hypervisor, qemu or spice-server change (master plan, "The shape, as agreed");
    • the .vv QUIC endpoint key. The prototype's client takes the endpoint from a command-line flag; kerbside/api.py's template is phase 8's work (decision 9);
    • any deployment change — no new ansible variable, no compose port, no docs/network-ports.md row. The feature is off in every build the project ships, so nothing is exposed (decision 8);
    • RFC 9221 datagrams. The master plan lists them as "room for later", and they are not needed to answer phase 7's questions;
    • 0-RTT resumption. Measured connect time is the 1-RTT case; 0-RTT is a phase 8 option with its own replay questions;
    • fixing anything the survey found wrong outside this phase's files. See Future work.

    What the survey found¶

    Checked against develop at 4ed7a31 on 2026-11-11. Two of the master plan's phase 7 claims did not survive, and two more needed grounding. The corrections are made at source in the master plan's phase 7 section and the docs/plans/index.md row as part of this phase's planning commit.

    1. The head-of-line-blocking argument is against a strawman¶

    The master plan argues (phase 7 section, "Streams without head-of-line blocking") that "multiplexing SPICE's channels over one TCP stream would make a keystroke wait behind a retransmitted display frame, on exactly the lossy links where the other benefits matter".

    That is true and it is not the comparison. Today Kerbside's client leg is one TCP connection per channel (rust/kerbside-proxy/src/listen.rs:179, one accept per channel; session.rs:159, one handle_connection per accepted stream), so inputs and display already travel on independent connections with independent loss recovery. Nobody has proposed a TCP multiplex. QUIC's stream independence therefore buys nothing against today's code; it only avoids a regression that a single-stream design would have introduced.

    What is left of the latency case is narrower: whether quinn's userspace loss recovery and congestion control beat the kernel's TCP for this traffic shape, and whether sharing one congestion controller across a session's channels is better or worse than N independent ones. That is a real question — one controller sees the whole session's bandwidth and cannot collectively overshoot, N controllers each probe independently — but it is not the slam dunk the section implies, and it could go either way.

    This changes what the phase decides. See decision 1.

    2. The rig cannot inject packet loss¶

    The master plan names the deciding measurement as latency "on the shaped-link rig's profiles plus 1-2% loss".

    tools/shaped-link/run-matrix.sh has no loss term. PROFILES parses as rtt_ms:rate_mbit (line 138, and the split at lines 618-620), and the qdisc it builds is netem delay <rtt/2>ms rate <N>mbit limit <pkts> (line 322). Nothing anywhere in the rig passes loss.

    So the phase's headline measurement needs a rig change before it can be taken at all. That is step 1, and it is the one piece of work the master plan's section does not anticipate.

    Two attendant details, both from the rig's own comments:

    • the rig forces gso_max_size 1500 gso_max_segs 1 and ethtool -K tso off gso off gro off on each veth end (lines 267-268) because "netem's queue limit counts packets, so it must see MTU-sized packets" (line 259). quinn's UDP path uses GSO. Whether gso_max_segs 1 segments UDP GSO writes before the qdisc the way it does TCP has to be verified by packet count, not assumed;
    • limit is computed in packets from the bandwidth-delay product (line 321). Loss interacts with that queue, so the loss runs are a new baseline, not a comparison against anything already recorded.

    3. The proxy has exactly one seam, and a QUIC stream fits it¶

    This is the finding that makes an end-to-end prototype cheap.

    • listen::run_secure is generic over its handler: F: Fn(SpiceStream, SocketAddr) -> Fut (rust/kerbside-proxy/src/listen.rs:169-178). The accept loop is the only TCP-specific code in it.
    • session::handle_connection(state, SpiceStream, SocketAddr) (session.rs:159) takes it from there: link message, caps check, RegisterChannel, keypair, ticket, authorize, then backend::run.
    • Everything downstream — the L0/L1 firewall (allowlist.rs, policy.rs), the relay (relay.rs), the ACK-window metrics (ackwindow.rs), the capability reply tables (caps.rs) and the session-termination registry (session.rs) — is written against SpiceStream, not against a socket.

    So a quinn accept loop that yields one SpiceStream per accepted bidirectional stream plugs in with no change to any of that, and the master plan's claim that "inside the proxy each QUIC stream becomes an ordinary SPICE channel before the firewall inspects it" is architecturally true as written.

    SpiceStream is ryll's, not Kerbside's: shakenfist-spice-protocol/src/link.rs:40, a three-variant enum (Plain(TcpStream), Tls(ClientTlsStream<TcpStream>), TlsServer(ServerTlsStream<TcpStream>)) with inherent read_exact/write_all/flush plus AsyncRead/AsyncWrite delegations. A fourth variant is the change, and it has to be made in ryll — hence a ryll pull request before the Kerbside one.

    4. quinn fits the proxy's runtime and packaging¶

    The master plan asks whether "a Rust QUIC stack (quinn is the obvious candidate) fits the proxy's runtime and packaging". Checked against the copy already in this host's cargo registry, quinn 0.11.12:

    • its default features are ["log", "platform-verifier", "runtime-tokio", "rustls-ring", "bloom"]. runtime-tokio matches the proxy's tokio multi-thread runtime, and rustls-ring matches both the proxy's rustls = { version = "0.23", features = ["ring"] } and the ring provider main installs process-wide. The resolved rustls in the proxy's lock is 0.23.46, which is quinn 0.11's requirement, so there would be one rustls in the tree and one crypto provider;
    • impl tokio::io::AsyncRead for RecvStream (quinn-0.11.12/src/recv_stream.rs:512) and impl tokio::io::AsyncWrite for SendStream (src/send_stream.rs:326) are both real, so tokio::io::join(recv, send) is a single AsyncRead + AsyncWrite + Unpin value — exactly what a SpiceStream variant needs;
    • nothing in that feature set builds C or assembly, so the maturin bin wheel is unaffected — unlike transcode, which is kept out of the wheel because it builds openh264 from source (rust/kerbside-proxy/Cargo.toml, the [features] comment);
    • ryll's Cargo.lock already resolves quinn 0.11.12 (pulled in by reqwest), and webrtc already pulls quinn-udp, so the ryll side adds no new build dependency at all.

    platform-verifier and bloom are not wanted — the first is a client-side trust store we do not use, the second an amplification defence for a public server. See decision 5.

    5. There is no UDP anywhere in Kerbside today¶

    docs/network-ports.md lists TCP 5900, TCP 5901, TCP 13002, loopback TCP 13003 and the control-plane unix socket, and nothing else. ansible/roles/kerbside/defaults/main.yml:41-42 exposes kerbside_vdi_secure_port and kerbside_vdi_insecure_port only. demo/docker-compose.yml publishes the same two.

    So the UDP exposure question the master plan asks phase 7 to "make or defer" is genuinely unmade, and no deployment artifact has a UDP hole to widen. Phase 3's WebRTC output and phase 4's transport input both ended no-go, so phase 7 is the first thing to actually need the decision. See decision 8.

    6. The audit trail records one row per channel, keyed on a per-channel UUID¶

    RegisterChannelRequest carries node, connection_ref, client_ip, client_port, connection_id, channel_type, channel_id (kerbside/rpc/kerbside.proto:187-195). connection_ref is a fresh UUID per channel (session.rs:160), and db.record_channel_info_by_ref (kerbside/db.py:781-799) upserts one ProxyChannel row per connection_ref.

    Under one QUIC connection per session, every channel of that session reports the same client_ip and client_port — the UDP four-tuple — where today each channel reports its own ephemeral TCP source port. And after a QUIC path migration those recorded values are stale, with no code path that would ever update them.

    Neither breaks anything: connection_ref keys the row, and nothing joins on client_port. But it changes what the audit trail means, and that is one of the questions the master plan asks phase 7 to answer. See decision 7.

    7. The rig already has the hooks the prototype needs¶

    Better news than expected, and it removes a step. run-matrix.sh takes PROXY_BIN (line 132), RYLL_ARGS (header line ~88) and a CONFIGS entry whose optional fourth field is "extra proxy flags separated by commas, each written --flag or --flag=value" — the transcoder's own precedent is tx:0:0:--transcode-display,--transcode-ack-window=40, with the note that "the proxy must be built with FEATURES=transcode for those".

    A QUIC config is therefore quic:0:0:--quic-port=5902 against a FEATURES=quic build, with no rig change beyond step 1's loss term. rust/kerbside-proxy/Makefile:22-26 already threads FEATURES into build, test and clippy.

    8. Everything else checked out¶

    The master plan's remaining phase 7 claims hold:

    • channels of one session can land on different proxy nodes (docs/proxy-architecture.md:805, and the distributed-deployment note repeated in session.rs's SessionRegistry doc comment), and session termination is a DB-mediated intent each node acts on;
    • the client leg has no client-certificate auth (rust/kerbside-proxy/src/tls.rs:43, .with_no_client_auth()), so a quinn server config can reuse tls.rs's PEM loaders;
    • docs/spice/console-vv-extensions.md exists and is the right home for a QUIC endpoint key when phase 8 defines one. It currently defines one extension, ticket-valid-until, which Kerbside's own template does not emit — noted in Future work, not fixed here;
    • finding 3's per-channel ticket re-check and late-channel ticket lifetime floor are backend-leg properties and are untouched by a client-leg transport change.

    One thing phase 1 recorded that phase 7 should not forget: TCP_NOTSENT_LOWAT on the client leg (--client-notsent-lowat) is TCP-only and means nothing on a QUIC leg. It defaults off, so nothing is lost; the write-up should say so rather than leave a reader wondering.

    9. The rig's ryll pin cannot carry new ryll work¶

    Found on 2026-11-12 while checking step 6's premises, after the plan was first written. It blocks step 6 as the plan was drafted, and the fix is a new step.

    tools/shaped-link/build-ryll.sh pins RYLL_REF to 0d140b3 and applies ryll-surface-drawn-rect.patch, which adds a rect field to the control socket's surface_drawn event. keydraw-latency.py needs that field to tell the guest's key-box draw apart from the screen activity's draws — without it the rig measures nothing. The script's own comment says the pin is fixed because "the patch stopped applying to ryll develop when control/server.rs moved after 0d140b3, so HEAD no longer builds".

    Confirmed against ryll develop at e381b1e: git apply --check ryll-surface-drawn-rect.patch fails at shakenfist-spice-renderer/src/control/server.rs:480, and surface_drawn does not appear in ryll's src/ at all.

    So the rig cannot build a ryll that carries both the rect patch and step 5's QUIC dialer, and step 6 has no client to measure with. The fix is the one build-ryll.sh already names: land the rect field in ryll's control socket protocol proper, drop the patch, and move the pin. That is ryll#673's rect half, already open and already listed in the master plan's Future work. See steps 5a and 5b.

    Decisions¶

    1. Latency is a floor for this phase, not the case for it. The master plan says the loss-and-busy latency measurement "is the measurement that decides the phase". Survey finding 1 says that measurement is now a comparison between two designs that both already avoid cross-channel head-of-line blocking, so a tie is the most likely outcome and a tie is not a reason to stop. The phase therefore decides on:

    • a latency floor: QUIC must not be materially worse than per-channel TCP under loss, where materially means outside the run-to-run spread the rig itself measures. A clear loss is a no-go;
    • the operational benefits, which are what the master plan's own case rests on and which only the prototype can price: one connection is one session behind a load balancer, one handshake instead of per-channel TCP+TLS+link+ticket, and a session that survives a path change;
    • the cost: a UDP port to expose, a second listener to maintain, and a fourth SpiceStream variant in a crate ryll and Kerbside share.

    This is the decision most likely to be argued with. The counter-argument is real: a phase whose headline measurement is expected to tie is a phase measuring the wrong thing, and the honest response would be to abandon phases 7 and 8 and spend the time on the four upstream items Future work says became the next plan after phase 4's no-go. I am not taking that option, for two reasons. First, the operational case stands on its own and has nothing to do with latency: the distributed-deployment constraint is a live piece of complexity in the proxy's design today, and "one connection is one session" removes it rather than working around it. Second, the latency question is not actually settled — one congestion controller per session against N per session is a genuine unknown, and step 2 prices it for a fraction of a phase. But if Michael would rather spend phases 7 and 8 on items 2, 6, 7 and 8 instead, this is the point to say so, and the back brief gates on it.

    2. Step 2 is a hard gate, and it is cheap. No SPICE integration work starts until a transport-only bench has compared N TCP connections against one QUIC connection with N streams, over the shaped link with loss. The bench carries no SPICE: one saturating stream plus one interactive request/response stream, which is the traffic shape that matters, with no guest, no qemu and no spice-server to add variance. It resolves differences the full rig cannot — the recorded p95 spread on the full rig reaches 71 ms at 150 ms / 2 Mbit (docs/performance/spice-server-patches.md:220), which is wider than any plausible transport effect.

    The gate: if QUIC's interactive p95 is worse than TCP's by more than the bench's own run-to-run spread, on either loss level of the 80 ms / 10 Mbit profile, the phase stops at step 2 and writes up a no-go. That is a legitimate and cheap ending.

    3. Past the gate, the prototype is end to end, not synthetic. A replay harness cannot answer the remaining questions, because spice-server reacts to the transport: its ACK window, its channel "blocked" state and its stream rate control all key off what the socket does (master plan, finding 1). Connect time and path-change behaviour are likewise properties of the real handshake sequence, not of a bench. Survey finding 3 says the integration is small, which is what makes this affordable.

    4. The prototype plugs in at handle_connection, and nothing else in the proxy changes. A quinn endpoint, an accept loop over connections and their bidirectional streams, each stream joined into a new SpiceStream::Quic variant and handed to the existing handler with the connection's current peer address. The firewall, relay, caps, ACK-window metrics and session registry are not touched. If a step finds itself editing relay.rs or policy.rs, that is a signal the design has gone wrong, and it stops for review.

    Both sides are behind default-off cargo features — quic in rust/kerbside-proxy/Cargo.toml, matching the transcode precedent, and a quic feature in ryll's protocol crate. The release wheel's build (tools/build-proxy-wheel.sh) is not to mention either.

    5. quinn, with its default features off. default-features = false, features = ["runtime-tokio", "rustls-ring", "log"]. platform-verifier is a client trust store this server does not need and pulls a dependency tree with it; bloom is an amplification defence worth having on a public endpoint but noise in an experiment, and phase 8 can turn it on with a reason. The server config is built TLS1.3-only with an ALPN of our own choosing, from tls.rs's existing PEM loaders — load_acceptor itself is not reusable, because quinn needs a rustls::ServerConfig rather than a TlsAcceptor, so tls.rs grows a sibling constructor behind the feature.

    6. The rig's loss term is rtt_ms:rate_mbit:loss_pct, with the third field optional. An omitted loss field must produce a qdisc line byte-identical to today's, so that every figure already recorded in docs/performance/ stays comparable and no existing invocation changes behaviour. Loss applies in both directions at the same percentage, which is what the rig's one-netem-per-veth-end structure gives for free, and netem's default uncorrelated per-packet model is what 1-2% means here.

    7. Channel identity stays the link message; the audit-trail consequence is recorded, not fixed. One QUIC stream carries one SPICE channel, and that stream's own link message states connection_id, channel type and channel id exactly as a TCP connection's does. So RegisterChannel is unchanged and connection_ref stays per-stream. Survey finding 6's two consequences — every channel of a session reporting the same client port, and those values going stale after a migration — go in the write-up as findings, with the recommendation that phase 8 record the QUIC connection id alongside them. Fixing the audit model is phase 8's work if there is a phase 8, and this plan does not pre-empt it.

    8. Phase 7 makes the UDP exposure recommendation, but lands no deployment change. The master plan asks phase 7 to make the decision or defer it to phase 3 or 4, both of which ended no-go, so there is nothing left to defer to. The write-up therefore states the recommendation — which port, why a separate one rather than 5900/udp, what it means for load balancers and NAT, and what an operator who cannot open UDP gets (today's behaviour, unchanged). It does not touch docs/network-ports.md, ansible or compose, because the feature is off in every shipped build and documenting an exposure that does not exist would be false.

    9. The .vv file is not touched. The prototype's ryll side takes --quic and an endpoint on the command line, which is all the rig needs (RYLL_ARGS exists for exactly this). Adding a conditional key to kerbside/api.py's flat VIRTVIEWER_TEMPLATE (line 463) and defining it in docs/spice/console-vv-extensions.md is phase 8's work. This keeps Python, the REST API and four .vv endpoints out of a spike entirely. The template does have the right shape for it when the time comes — %(tls_port)s, %(ca_cert)s and %(host_subject)s are each a prebuilt string or empty.

    10. The time box is the step table, with one stop rule. Seven steps, of which 3 to 7 run only past step 2's gate. If step 6's matrix has not produced a complete set of numbers after two full passes, step 7 writes up what there is and says which cells are missing. A spike that overruns into a third matrix pass is a spike that has stopped being a spike.

    Risks and mitigations¶

    • The congestion controllers confound the comparison. quinn defaults to Cubic and runs its controller in userspace; the kernel's TCP uses whatever is configured, and phase 1 found this host has no tcp_bbr module loadable from an unprivileged namespace. A QUIC-BBR against TCP-Cubic number would be meaningless. Mitigation: step 2's brief pins both arms to the same algorithm for the headline and runs any BBR arm separately and labelled. The management session checks the recorded controller on both sides before accepting step 2's numbers.
    • UDP GSO defeats netem's packet accounting, silently making the QUIC arm look better or worse than it is. Mitigation: step 1 verifies packet counts for UDP explicitly and reports the method; step 2 disables quinn's segmentation offload if it does not hold. The management session rejects step 1 if the verification is asserted rather than measured.
    • The spread swallows the result. The full rig's p95 spread reaches 71 ms. Mitigation: the deciding comparison is step 2's bench, which carries no guest and no spice-server; the end-to-end runs in step 6 are for the operational questions and for the floor, and step 7 is required to say where the spread was too wide to tell.
    • The ryll pin couples the repositories. The proxy pins the ryll crates by exact rev, and dev-proxy-wheel.yml relies on that to decide whether to publish. Mitigation: step 3's pull request merges before step 4 bumps the pins, in one commit, with no path override at any point. The management session checks Cargo.toml and Cargo.lock agree and that the three revs match.
    • A feature flag that is not really off. A quic feature that leaks into the wheel would ship a UDP listener nobody asked for. Mitigation: the definition of done requires cargo tree -i quinn to be empty on a default build and tools/build-proxy-wheel.sh to be free of the string quic; both are checked by the management session, not just claimed.
    • Scope creep into phase 8. The .vv key, the audit-trail change and the deployment work are all one commit away at every step. Mitigation: decisions 7, 8 and 9 say no in writing, and step 4's brief lists the files not to touch. A step that edits kerbside/api.py or ansible/ is wrong by construction.
    • The phase answers "it ties" and nobody is better off. Mitigation: that is an acceptable outcome, written down as such in decision 1, and the back brief gates on whether Michael wants it bought at all before step 3 spends anything.
