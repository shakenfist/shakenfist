    A SPICE helper on qemu's D-Bus display, on a shaped link¶

    This page compares two ways of serving a qemu guest's console over SPICE through Kerbside's proxy:

    • qemu's own spice-server, with streaming-video=off and with streaming-video=all;
    • andris, a standalone SPICE server, attached to qemu's D-Bus display (-display dbus,p2p=on) with its qemu platform, as a helper process next to qemu.

    The proxy relays both in exactly the same way. It uses the shaped-link rig, the guest, the keypress-to-draw metric and the image-quality scoring of the transcoding measurements, with the same link profiles, so the tables line up with that page's. Measured on 2026-10-29.

    Summary¶

    • Where andris streams, the helper's latency is level with streaming-video=all. On the unshaped, 20 ms / 50 Mbit and 80 ms / 50 Mbit links, with busy and heavy activity, and at 80 ms / 10 Mbit with busy activity, the helper's p50 was within 5 ms of all's, and its p95 within 7 ms. With heavy activity on the 50 Mbit links it was 5 to 6 times faster than streaming-video=off: 43 against 250 ms at 20 ms / 50 Mbit and 98 against 462 ms at 80 ms / 50 Mbit.
    • Where andris does not stream, the helper is the slowest of the three. andris sends a moving area as H.264 only after 20 frames of change within 2 s, and on the slower links its ACK window held it below that rate, so it sent the activity as JPEG tiles that filled the link. At 80 ms / 10 Mbit heavy its p50 was 3492 ms, against 2039 ms with streaming off and 113 ms with all. At 80 ms / 5 Mbit it was 3263 ms (busy) and 6931 ms (heavy), against 371 and 4952 ms with streaming off. At 150 ms / 2 Mbit no helper repeat drew its first key press within the rig's 15 s limit, in four tries of each activity. Neither did the relay with streaming off with heavy activity.
    • Idle, the helper draws a key press sooner. Its p50 was 3 ms on the unshaped link, against 15 and 19 ms for spice-server, and 88 ms at 80 ms / 10 Mbit, against 96 and 98 ms.
    • qemu plus andris costs about what qemu with spice-server costs, except with busy activity on fast links, where it costs up to twice as much as qemu with streaming-video=all. With activity, qemu itself used 0.08 to 0.10 cores behind the helper, against 0.12 to 0.68 with streaming off and 0.11 to 0.18 with all. andris added 0.004 cores idle, 0.10 to 0.11 for a heavy activity it streamed, and 0.17 to 0.23 for a busy one, which it encodes as a larger H.264 stream. Idle, the sum was 0.018 to 0.022 cores against 0.017 to 0.021. With heavy activity on a LAN it was 0.195 cores, against 0.677 with streaming off and 0.173 with all. With busy activity on the 50 Mbit links it was 0.33, against 0.18 to 0.22.
    • Memory moves from qemu to the helper. qemu's peak RSS was 181 to 194 MiB without spice-server, against 207 to 228 MiB with it. andris peaked at 34 to 66 MiB, so the pair's peaks summed to 222 to 250 MiB.
    • The helper sends a tenth of the display traffic where it streams. 0.9 to 2.2 Mbit/s, against 5.5 to 48 Mbit/s for all and up to 151 Mbit/s for streaming off.
    • The helper puts lossy pixels on the text next to the activity, and repairs them. Mid-animation, 53248 background pixels next to the activity differed from the reference in every helper repeat: the 64-pixel tiles along the activity's left and top edges. Over the whole background that was 41.7 dB for a busy activity andris streamed, 33.0 to 36.5 dB for a heavy one, and 39.3 to 39.4 dB where it sent JPEG tiles. all left its 16-pixel strip (7680 pixels, 46.4 to 47.2 dB). Every helper repeat's frozen frame matched the lossless reference exactly.
    • The std-vga row costs qemu 0.77 cores whatever the guest draws. qemu polls std-vga's dirty bitmap every 30 ms and copies each changed band over the D-Bus socket. Its latency followed the virtio-gpu helper's, 22 to 30 ms slower where andris streamed, and like it, it was seconds behind at 80 ms / 10 Mbit heavy (4400 ms p50, one repeat of two).
    • No server crashed. No qemu or andris exited in any of the 108 matrix repeats or the 10 reference repeats, and spice-server 0.15.2's crash on connecting while streams exist did not recur. The 13 repeats lost were all first key presses that took over 15 s. See Caveats.

    Configurations¶

    Name Display server qemu display Pacing
    SPICE, streaming off spice-server in qemu -spice, streaming-video=off spice-server's ACK window
    SPICE, streaming all spice-server in qemu -spice, streaming-video=all spice-server's ACK window
    helper andris, --platform qemu -display dbus,p2p=on, no -spice andris's ACK window (20)
    std-vga helper as helper as helper, with qemu's std-vga as helper

    Every case runs through the proxy, relayed, with no proxy flags, and with the same Ryll. andris ran with its defaults: frames at least 33 ms apart, JPEG quality 85 for photographic tiles with a lossless resend after 0.5 s of stillness, and H.264 streams for areas it judges to be video. It attaches through qemu's QMP socket, takes picture and damage through qemu's shared-memory Unix.Map interface on virtio-gpu, and listens where spice-server would, with the same ticket.

    The guest is the rig's keydraw guest, as in the transcoding measurements: 1280x800, a 96x96 key box, and a 640x480 activity rectangle at (624, 160). Idle draws nothing but the key box; busy animates a smooth gradient with 3 bits of noise at 30 fps; heavy uses 8 bits of noise. The guest runs on virtio-gpu 2D (virtio-vga) except in the std-vga rows, where it runs on qemu's std-vga, driven by bochs-drm. On virtio-gpu its damage path is DRM_IOCTL_MODE_DIRTYFB. bochs-drm has no dirty-framebuffer hook, so on std-vga qemu finds the damage itself, from VRAM's dirty bitmap.

    Cases with no completed repeat have no cost figures, since CPU is measured over the key presses.

    qemu. Without spice-server, qemu used 0.075 to 0.104 cores with activity, much the same on every link, because it no longer compresses anything: on virtio-gpu it shares the guest's framebuffer with andris and sends it only each flush's rectangle over the D-Bus socket. With spice-server it used more on fast links, where it sent more: 0.31 to 0.68 cores with streaming off on a LAN, 0.16 to 0.17 with all.

    andris. andris's CPU follows what it encodes. A live H.264 stream of the 640x480 activity costs it 0.17 to 0.23 cores for busy activity and 0.10 to 0.11 for heavy. The stream ran at 525 to 910 kbit/s, except with heavy activity at 80 ms / 50 Mbit, where andris's encoder held it to 290 kbit/s. When it sends JPEG tiles instead, as on the slow links, the link limits how many it encodes, and its CPU falls to 0.04 to 0.06 cores.

    The proxy relays both alike: 0.001 to 0.08 cores. It used less with the helper, because the helper sent less.

    Memory. Without spice-server, qemu's peak RSS was 19 to 43 MiB smaller. andris's peak, 34 to 66 MiB, holds its copy of the framebuffer and its shadow (4 MiB each at 1280x800), its encoders, and the stream's state.

    Every session that streamed sent 20 JPEG draws of the activity's first frames before its stream began. andris starts a stream on an area whose 64-pixel tiles have each changed in 20 frames within 2 s, with no gap over 200 ms, and are mostly photographic. Where the link could not carry those first JPEG frames as fast as the guest drew them, andris's ACK window throttled it below that rate, so no tile ever qualified, and it went on sending every frame of the activity as JPEG tiles into a link that could not carry them. Its ACK lag then grew to seconds, and the key box's draws queued behind those tiles.

    Image quality¶

    The reference is the frozen frame of an unshaped case relayed from spice-server with streaming off, where every draw is lossless. It was taken twice per activity, and the two captures were byte-identical for each of idle, busy and heavy. A std-vga reference, taken the same way for busy and heavy, was byte-identical to the virtio-gpu one, so the std-vga rows are scored against the same reference.

    During animation. andris works in 64-pixel tiles. As on the transcoding page, the activity's left and top edges, at x 624 and y 160, do not fall on tile boundaries, so the tiles along them are mostly text. andris's stream covers whole tiles, 704x512 at (576, 128), and so do its JPEG tiles, so 48 pixels to the left of the activity and 32 above it go lossy with it: 53248 pixels. PSNR over the whole static background (707584 pixels) was 41.7 dB for a busy activity andris streamed and 33.0 to 36.5 dB for a heavy one, whose noise its H.264 encoder spread onto the text at under 1 Mbit/s. Where it sent JPEG tiles, at quality 85, it was 39.3 to 39.4 dB. With all, spice-server's streams are 32 pixels wide, and the 16-pixel strip to the left of the activity is lossy (7680 pixels, 46.4 to 47.2 dB over the background). With streaming off the background stays exact.

    std-vga¶

    On std-vga qemu has no shared buffer to hand andris. It polls the dirty bitmap every 30 ms and sends each changed band of whole scanlines over the D-Bus socket. qemu used 0.76 to 0.78 cores in every std-vga case, against 0.08 to 0.10 for the same activity on virtio-gpu, and andris 0.13 to 0.23 with a stream and 0.06 without. The bands are full-width, so andris diffed them against its shadow, and in most sessions sent 800 to 2600 JPEG draws where it sent 20 on virtio-gpu. It still streamed the activity where the link allowed, and its latency at 80 ms / 10 Mbit busy was 135 ms p50, 29 ms behind the virtio-gpu helper.

    Caveats¶

    • The client decoded andris's streams, and spice-server's that it accepted. andris sent H.264, which Ryll decoded: 10442 frames received and 10442 decoded, in the 14 streams of the virtio-gpu helper repeats. spice-server with streaming-video=all sent MJPEG, not H.264, in every case. It offered 21 streams at once, one for each 32-pixel column of the activity (with heavy activity at 80 ms / 5 Mbit and 150 ms / 2 Mbit it also ended and recreated them, 36 and 983 in one repeat each). Ryll accepted 16, its limit on open streams, and refused the rest, and it decoded every frame of the streams it accepted (302549 of 302549). So with all Ryll did not decode five columns of the activity while it moved, and spice-server's rate control had reports from 16 of its 21 streams. The key box is never streamed, so its latency is unaffected. The earlier pages on this rig, with the same Ryll and the same proxy, report spice-server's streams as H.264 that Ryll did not decode. Why spice-server chose MJPEG here was not established.
    • The guest flushes its whole plane. The guest's kernel (Linux 6.12, with the stock drm_kms_helper) carries the bug that ignores damage clips, so every flush from this guest covers all 1280x800 pixels, whatever it drew. A runtime trace of qemu's virtio_gpu_cmd_res_flush event against andris's listener showed this, and showed qemu passing each flush on unchanged. This holds for every configuration. qemu's spice-display diffs each update against its own copy in 32-pixel columns before spice-server sees it; with the D-Bus display, andris diffs in 64-pixel tiles instead.
    • Host load. This host is shared. The one-minute load average had a median of 1.3 over the run and passed 16, the number of hardware threads, for 8% of it, peaking at 27, all between 19:44 and 20:13. That window covered the references, the std-vga row and the first repeat of each unshaped case. No repeat was lost to it, and none showed the client stall that the transcoding page excluded: the client leg's rwnd_limited share was under 50% in every repeat. LAN latency moves with load, which is why the second, quiet repeats are shown separately. The std-vga row's unshaped cases had run entirely inside it, so they were run again at a load of about 2 and pooled. The load average at the start and end of each invocation:
    Invocation Start End
    references (virtio-gpu) 4.2 7.5
    references (std-vga) 5.5 11.4

    - First key presses over 15 s. The rig gives up on a case whose probe
    press draws nothing within 15 s. At 150 ms / 2 Mbit that happened in all
    four repeats of the helper, busy and heavy, and of the relay with
    streaming off, heavy, at load averages of 0.6 to 1.5. andris reported
    ACK lags of 7 and 11 s there. The transcoding page measured the relay
    with streaming off at a p50 of 13.6 s on that cell, so its first press
    is near the limit. The same happened in one std-vga repeat at
    80 ms / 10 Mbit heavy, at load 22.
    - No crashes. No qemu or andris exited during a case. spice-server
    0.15.2's crash on connecting while streams exist did not recur in any
    all repeat, with the activity delayed 8 s, or 12 s on the second
    150 ms / 2 Mbit run.
    - Repeat counts are small. Two repeats of 40 presses per cell, four at
    150 ms / 2 Mbit.
    - The helper's listener has no lag watchdog. If andris stopped reading,
    qemu would queue its display messages without bound on std-vga and on a
    guest that flips pages. qemu's peak RSS in the std-vga cases, 193 to
    200 MiB, shows that no such queue built up here: the std-vga row's
    slow-link latency comes from andris's ACK window, not from qemu.

    Procedure¶

    The rig is tools/shaped-link/, described in Testing; the header of run-matrix.sh documents every variable, including the helper display, ANDRIS_BIN and GUEST_VGA. Builds:

    make -C rust/kerbside-proxy release
tools/shaped-link/build-ryll.sh /tmp/dh/build     # ryll 0d140b3 plus the rect patch
tools/shaped-link/build-andris.sh /tmp/dh/build   # andris 440b782, qemu feature
tools/shaped-link/build-guest.sh /tmp/dh/guest

    Common settings for every run:

    export PROXY_BIN=rust/kerbside-proxy/target/release/kerbside-proxy \
    RYLL_BIN=/tmp/dh/build/ryll/target/release/ryll \
    ANDRIS_BIN=/tmp/dh/build/andris/target/release/andris \
    MOCK_PYTHON=.tox/py3/bin/python QUALITY=1 \
    ACTIVITY_DELAY=8 WARMUP=10 REPEATS=2 SAMPLES=40
G=/tmp/dh/guest Q=qemu-system-x86_64

    The references, unshaped and relayed with streaming off, twice per activity, then compared with cmp:

    DISPLAYS="off:$Q:off:$G" CONFIGS=relay:0:0 PROFILES=0:0 \
ACTIVITIES='idle:0:0 busy:30:3 heavy:30:8' WORKDIR=/tmp/dh/ref \
    tools/shaped-link/run-matrix.sh
GUEST_VGA=std DISPLAYS="stdoff:$Q:off:$G" CONFIGS=relay:0:0 PROFILES=0:0 \
ACTIVITIES='busy:30:3 heavy:30:8' WORKDIR=/tmp/dh/stdref \
    tools/shaped-link/run-matrix.sh

    The std-vga row:

    GUEST_VGA=std DISPLAYS="stdhelper:$Q:helper:$G" CONFIGS=relay:0:0 \
PROFILES='0:0 80:10' ACTIVITIES='busy:30:3 heavy:30:8' WORKDIR=/tmp/dh/std \
    tools/shaped-link/run-matrix.sh

    The matrix, as two invocations because idle runs on two links only:

    export DISPLAYS="off:$Q:off:$G all:$Q:all:$G helper:$Q:helper:$G" CONFIGS=relay:0:0
PROFILES='0:0 80:10' ACTIVITIES='idle:0:0 busy:30:3 heavy:30:8' \
    WORKDIR=/tmp/dh/main-a tools/shaped-link/run-matrix.sh
PROFILES='20:50 80:50 80:5 150:2' ACTIVITIES='busy:30:3 heavy:30:8' \
    WORKDIR=/tmp/dh/main-b tools/shaped-link/run-matrix.sh

    Three cells at 150 ms / 2 Mbit lost every repeat to a first press over 15 s. That link was run again in full, into a third WORKDIR, with ACTIVITY_DELAY=12 WARMUP=14, as the transcoding page's rerun was; the same three cells failed the same way. The std-vga row's unshaped link, whose repeats had all run during the load spike, was also run again, into a fourth WORKDIR, once the host was quiet. The tables pool every completed repeat. A repeat would count as stalled, and be excluded, if the last ss snapshot of the proxy's client-leg display socket showed rwnd_limited over 50%; none did. Then:

    tools/shaped-link/quality.sh cases \
    --reference /tmp/dh/ref/0ms-0mbit/busy/off-relay-r1/frozen.png /tmp/dh/main-*/*/busy
# ... and the same for heavy and idle, each with its own reference
tools/shaped-link/summarise.py /tmp/dh/pooled

    The whole run took about 2 h 40 min: the 36 cases of the first matrix invocation 40 minutes, the 48 of the second 1 h 15 min, and the slowest cases, at 80 ms / 5 Mbit and 150 ms / 2 Mbit, 3 to 5 minutes each. dbus-helper/summary.md is summarise.py's full output over the pooled repeats, and dbus-helper/andris-sessions.txt is andris's session summary for each helper repeat.
