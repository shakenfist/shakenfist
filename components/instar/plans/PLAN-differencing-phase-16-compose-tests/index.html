    Differencing phase 16: composition tests and fuzz¶

    Prompt¶

    Plan phase 16 of PLAN-differencing.md. Phase 15 landed in f14554cf (#646) and closed the op-consistency boundary; phases 11 to 15 built and rolled out composition, and every test that holds them is either a crate test over a synthetic fixture or an integration test over one of three real chains compared against a golden the project generated itself.

    This phase is the external check on all of it: cross-validation against the phase 1 oracle for chains instar wrote and chains it did not, the two fixture-blocked coverage gaps phase 15 handed over (#631 and #635), the 4096-byte-sector geometry phase 13 left behind, and coverage fuzzing of the compose path, which no fuzz target reaches today.

    The phase plan is the deliverable; implementation is a separate ask.

    Planning effort¶

    High. The master plan's phase 16 section makes four factual claims about tooling and two of them are wrong, in ways that change the shape of the phase rather than a line number — one of the two routes it contrasts does not exist in the packaged libvhdi at all. The 4K gap it inherits turns out to be unreachable from an integration fixture for an arithmetic reason, and settling that needed the chunk-ratio sums done rather than asserted. The oracle's agreement with the project's own goldens had to be measured before anything could be built on it, and it disagrees in exactly one place.

    Review effort: medium. The master plan does not specify one. No production code changes in this phase except the shared fuzz harness; the judgement is in this document and in what the oracle is allowed to disagree about.

    Scope¶

    In scope.

    • Oracle cross-validation of composed data. The existing TestDifferencingLibvhdiOracle checks that libvhdi reads instar's child as naming its parent — an identity check on metadata. Nothing compares a composed byte. This phase adds a third independent reader of the same chains.
    • The write-then-read loop over instar's own output. create -b writes a differencing child; nothing ever reads one back. Phase 16 composes an instar-written chain with both instar and libvhdi and compares all three answers (child, parent, oracle).
    • Issue #635: no integration fixture for a parentless differencing VHDX, so the composing operations' "no parent behind it" refusal is driven end to end for VHD only.
    • Issue #631: no CLI-level coverage of a differencing-over- differencing chain. Both formats, with golden compositions.
    • The 4096-byte logical sector geometry. Crate level, for the reasons in decision 3.
    • A compose fuzz target, and the multi-device mock call table it needs.
    • tools/mutate-differencing.sh cases for every property added here, docs/testing.md, and CHANGELOG.md.
    • src/.devcontainer/Dockerfile: python3-libvhdi.
    • New fixtures and their generator entries, committed to instar-testdata (decision 5).

    Out of scope, with the reasons stated.

    • Issue #628 — create -b refuses a differencing backing for a reason phase 14 falsified. It asks a question this phase cannot answer with a test: whether every output format create can write has a sensible relationship with a differencing backing image. It is a decision and then possibly a feature, and it also blocks nothing here — decision 5 builds the grandchild fixtures from bytes, not by asking instar to stack a child on a child.
    • Issue #632 — safe-mode rebase onto a raw new backing fails as an unparseable overlay header. A real defect with a wrong message, found during phase 14, and unrelated to differencing: it reproduces with a qcow2 old chain. Not this phase's.
    • Issues #641, #642, #643 — chain composition in map and measure, and whether check and measure should still refuse. Phase 15 filed all three as hand-offs; nothing here changes what any operation composes.
    • Documentation of the composition story — phase 17. This phase touches docs/testing.md because it adds tests, and CHANGELOG.md because it adds fixtures and a fuzz target. The read-side rows of docs/format-coverage.md, docs/chain-discovery.md and docs/chain-config.md stay with 17.
    • Differential fuzzing against libvhdi. differential-fuzz.yml compares instar with the libyal parsers on single images. Extending it to chains means teaching the harness to author a chain per iteration and is a plan's worth of work; the coverage fuzz target in 16f is the cheaper half and the one the master plan asked for.

    What the survey found¶

    The master plan's phase 16 bullet (docs/plans/PLAN-differencing.md, the Phase 16, tests and fuzz item) was written on 2026-09-05, before phases 11 to 15 executed. All references below are against f14554cf, and the measurements were taken on 2026-10-11.

    The CLI export it contrasts does not exist¶

    The bullet says the harness "drives the python3-libvhdi binding with an explicit set_parent() rather than a CLI export". The conclusion is right and the reason is not: Debian 13's libvhdi-utils 20240509-2+b1 ships exactly two binaries, vhdiinfo and vhdimount. There is no vhdiexport to reject. The real alternative to the binding is vhdimount, a FUSE mount, which is a worse thing to require of CI than a Python module.

    Measured by extracting the package rather than by reading the upstream source: the .deb contains /usr/bin/vhdiinfo, /usr/bin/vhdimount, libvhdi.so.1.0.0 and a man page for vhdiinfo alone.

    python3-libvhdi is a separate package and is not installed in the devcontainer. src/.devcontainer/Dockerfile:39 installs libvhdi-utils, which is why vhdiinfo is available to tests/base.py:_require_vhdiinfo and the binding is not.

    The binding has what the phase needs. Probed against the extracted module (pyvhdi 20240509, built for CPython 3.13, which matches the host and the container):

    file methods: bytes_per_sector, close, disk_type, format_version,
get_bytes_per_sector, get_disk_type, get_format_version,
get_identifier, get_media_size, get_offset, get_parent_filename,
get_parent_identifier, identifier, media_size, open, open_file_object,
parent_filename, parent_identifier, read, read_buffer,
read_buffer_at_offset, seek, seek_offset, set_parent, signal_abort,
tell

    The oracle agrees with the goldens, except in one sector¶

    This is the premise the whole phase rests on, so it was measured before anything was planned. Driving pyvhdi.file.set_parent() over the three real chains in instar-testdata/custom/format-coverage/ and comparing the composed bytes with the recorded *-composed.raw goldens:

    chain result
    vhd-diff-child-aligned.vhd MATCH, all 16777216 bytes
    vhdx-diff-child.vhdx MATCH, all 16777216 bytes
    vhd-diff-child-mixed.vhd differs in 512 bytes: sector 2 alone

    The divergence is the libvhdi VHD sector-bitmap defect the master plan already records under Phases 8 and 16 (libvhdi_block_descriptor_read_sector_bitmap_data decodes the per-block bitmap with an unmasked shift). Its shape here is worth writing down, because the master plan's description does not quite predict it: the oracle returns zeros where the golden holds PARENT-sector-000002. That is consistent — reading the sector as "present in the child" means reading the child's block, which was never written there — but a test author expecting the child's data would look for the wrong bytes.

    One sector, deterministic, on the one fixture built to put parent-owned and child-owned sectors in the same bitmap byte. vhd-diff-child-aligned is the fixture built to avoid that, and the oracle agrees with it exactly, which is the control that tells us the defect and not the harness is responsible.

    The composed goldens are independent, and the generator is in this repo¶

    tests/test_differencing.py:18 credits scripts/create-vhd-testdata.sh with the goldens. That is accurate, and the script is in instar, not in instar-testdata — 1318 lines, building VHD dynamic headers and patching VHDX metadata in embedded Python. tests/manifest.json records generated_by for each fixture. So the goldens never passed through instar's read path, and extending the generator is this repo's work even though the fixtures land in the other one.

    The script hardcodes SECTOR = 512 and derives chunk_ratio = (0x800000 * SECTOR) // block_size from it (:1006).

    The 4K gap is narrower than stated, and partly unreachable¶

    The bullet says "every multi-group and partial-group VHDX compose test runs at 512-byte logical sectors". One 4K case now exists — vhdx_map_and_measure_refuse_a_block_inside_a_region_at_4k_sectors (src/crates/qcow2/src/lib.rs:10552), added during phase 15's second review round — and it is build_vhdx_image(4096, false, 1, ...) with a single block: one chunk group, no stride, no padded bound. It is the only call in the tree that passes 4096. So the gap is real and it is specifically the multi-group and partial-group shapes, exactly as written.

    What the bullet could not know is that no integration fixture can close it. chunk_ratio = 2^23 × logical_sector_size / block_size, so at 4096 with the crate fixture's 1 MiB block it is 32768, and reaching a second chunk group needs 65536 payload blocks — a 64 GiB virtual disk. A crate fixture declares that size and materialises only the blocks it lists; a real file would have to carry a 512 KiB BAT. And nothing available authors a 4K-logical-sector VHDX at any size:

    • qemu-img create -f vhdx -o help on 10.0.13 offers block_size, block_state_zero, log_size, size and subformat. No sector size. Measured.
    • instar create has no logical_sector_size key either — the vhdx -o parser takes block_size and nothing sector-shaped (src/vmm/src/main.rs:14938, :14962), and src/crates/create/src/lib.rs never mentions logical_sector_size.

    Hence decision 3.

    Nothing fuzzes compose, and the harness is single-device by construction¶

    42 fuzz targets, listed in src/fuzz/Cargo.toml as [[bin]] entries and again in .github/workflows/coverage-fuzz.yml's TARGETS array. Both counts are 42 today and nothing cross-checks them — tools/ci/count-fuzz-targets.sh counts the Cargo entries only, to size the duration cap. grep -l 'ChainStates\|read_chain_virtual_cluster\| init_chain_states' src/fuzz/fuzz_targets/ returns nothing.

    fuzz_vhd_parent and fuzz_vhdx_parent fuzz the locator parsers, which is phase 9's work and a different input space, as the bullet says.

    Decisions¶

    1. The oracle is the pyvhdi binding, made mandatory in CI the same way vhdiinfo is. There is no CLI export to prefer or reject (survey above), and vhdimount would put a FUSE mount in the test path. python3-libvhdi goes into src/.devcontainer/Dockerfile beside libvhdi-utils, and tests/base.py gains a _require_pyvhdi honouring the existing INSTAR_REQUIRE_LIBVHDI variable rather than a second one: the two come from the same upstream release and a container missing one is missing both. A skipped oracle and a passing oracle look identical in a green run, and this repository has shipped a green workflow that ran nothing (autofix_green_runs_ran_nothing).

    2. The one-sector VHD divergence is asserted as a pinned exception, not skipped and not smoothed over. The cross-validation test compares the oracle's composition with instar's byte for byte, and carries a single named exception: sector 2 of vhd-diff-child-mixed, where libvhdi is expected to return 512 zero bytes and instar the parent's PARENT-sector-000002 pattern. The test fails if that sector agrees, if any other sector disagrees, or if the oracle's wrong bytes are anything other than zeros.

    The alternatives are worse in a way worth stating, because this is the decision most likely to be argued with. Skipping vhd-diff-child-mixed would drop the only fixture that exercises mixed ownership within a bitmap byte, which is the hardest thing the VHD reader does. Comparing only the sectors both agree about would make the test insensitive to the oracle's defect growing. Asserting "at most one sector differs" would pass if the wrong sector differed. Pinning it means that if libvhdi is ever fixed upstream, the devcontainer bump that picks the fix up turns this test red and someone deletes four lines with a clear conscience — which is the correct outcome, and the only one of the four that notices.

    1. 4K-sector coverage stays at crate level. Multi-group at 4096 needs a 64 GiB virtual disk, and no tool in the project or outside it authors a 4K-logical-sector VHDX at all (survey above). The fixture builder already takes the sector size as its first argument and build_vhdx_image_blocks already takes a payload block count for partial groups, so the coverage is a handful of new cases rather than new infrastructure. The BAT region caps it: VHDX_FIX_BAT_LENGTH is 1 MiB, so a differencing fixture at 4096 fits group_count up to 3 (3 × (32768 + 1) × 8 = 786456), and 4 trips the builder's own assert.

    The alternative — synthesising a 4K fixture by patching the metadata item, which the generator already does for HasParent — was considered and rejected for the multi-group case on the arithmetic, and as redundant for the single-group case: a 16 MiB image at 4096 has 8 payload blocks against a chunk ratio of 16384, so it can only ever express one partial group, which is what the existing 4K crate test already covers.

    1. One compose fuzz target, and the mock call table keeps its present behaviour by default. fuzz_chain_compose drives init_chain_states then read_chain_virtual_cluster over a two-device chain built from the fuzz input. The harness gains an opt-in multi-device mode; set_fuzz_input and build_call_table keep returning a one-device table, because 42 existing targets depend on that and a target that silently became two-device would lose coverage without failing.

    2. New fixtures are generated by extending scripts/create-vhd-testdata.sh, and the grandchild is built from bytes. #631 suggests a Hyper-V-authored grandchild; no Hyper-V is available, and the generator already hand-builds VHD dynamic headers and patches VHDX metadata, including the HasParent bit at :989. Fixtures land in instar-testdata with a direct commit to main (that repository takes no merge requests), rebasing first because the fuzz automation moves main daily, and tests/manifest.json records the generator for each.

    Building the grandchild rather than asking instar create -b to stack a child on a child also sidesteps #628 entirely, which is why that issue blocks nothing here.

    1. Cross-validation is additive. The golden comparisons stay exactly as they are. An oracle is a second opinion, not a replacement for an expectation the project can state itself — and the goldens are the stronger of the two, being independent of both readers.

    Risks and mitigations¶

    • The oracle becomes a mandatory CI dependency, and a packaging change can turn the suite red for a reason unrelated to instar. Decision 1 accepts that deliberately — the alternative is a skip nobody notices. Mitigation: the hard failure is tied to the existing INSTAR_REQUIRE_LIBVHDI variable rather than a new one, so there is one switch and one place to look; and the skip message names the package to install, as _require_vhdiinfo already does. If Debian ever drops python3-libvhdi, the failure says so.

    • 16b may find libvhdi cannot read what instar writes. The padded differencing BAT (#623) is instar's own interpretation of the spec and nothing third-party has read one. Mitigation: the step's brief makes the measurement the deliverable either way, with a documented exception in decision 2's shape and a new defect letter in the oracle docstring if the fault is libvhdi's. If instead the fault is ours, that is a defect found by exactly the cross-validation this phase exists to add, and it should be filed and fixed rather than absorbed into a test that avoids it.

    • 16d's fixtures are the expensive, blocking part. Two new generators, four new files, a second repository, and a bitmap-byte constraint that is easy to violate and whose symptom looks like an instar bug. Mitigation: the constraint is stated in the brief with the measured reason; the generator already carries the identity patching the grandchild needs twice; and 16a lands first so that the oracle is available to check the new fixtures as they are built rather than afterwards.

    • A test phase can grow without bound. Every one of these steps could be twice as large. Mitigation: each closes a numbered issue or a named gap from the master plan, and the definition of done states a figure for each rather than "coverage improved". The two issues phase 15 handed over are the scope, not a starting point.

    • The fuzz harness change touches all 42 existing targets. Mitigation: default behaviour is unchanged by construction (decision 4), and the step is accepted only once a second existing target has been built and run briefly to confirm it still reaches its usual coverage.

    • The repository's standing warning. Agents in this project assert plausible-but-wrong tool and format capabilities — three times in one phase, which is why qemu_capability_claims_sourcing_rule exists. Every claim in this phase about what libvhdi, qemu-img or the generator can do must come from running it. This plan's survey measured each of the ones it relies on, including by extracting a .deb to see which binaries it actually contains.

    Back brief¶

    Before implementation starts, confirm:

    1. Decision 1, before 16a. Making the Python binding a hard CI requirement adds a Debian package to the critical path of every integration run. It is the cheapest honest option and it is still a new dependency; if it should instead be a soft skip, say so now, because 16a and 16b are both written against a hard requirement and the definition of done asserts it.

    2. Decision 2, and the shape of the exception. Pinning a known upstream defect into a test is a deliberate trade: the test knows something that is true of libvhdi 20240509 and may not be true of the next release. The alternative readings are argued in the decision; if the preference is to skip the fixture instead, 16a shrinks and the VHD reader loses its only mixed-ownership cross-check.

    3. Scope of #631 against its cost. 16d is the largest step and the only one needing a second repository. It is in scope because phase 15 handed it over explicitly and because a two-level-only test cannot catch a reader that stops descending. If it should be deferred to a fixtures phase of its own, that is a defensible call and it should be made before 16a rather than after, since 16a's three-way comparison is written to extend over the grandchildren.

    4. Step order. 16a first — 16b, 16d and the definition of done all depend on the oracle existing. 16c is independent. 16e and 16f touch no fixture and can run at any point. 16g last by construction.

    5. Model and effort. 16a, 16b, 16d and 16f are opus at high effort: three of them measure external tools whose behaviour is not documented anywhere we control, and the fourth changes a harness 42 targets share. 16c and 16e are sonnet at medium with the briefs above. 16g is sonnet at medium rather than low, because the mutation harness's case accounting has drifted before. No step wants fable.

    6. The standing warning, restated for this phase. Measure, do not reason, about what libvhdi can read and what the generator can author. The survey for this plan corrected two tooling claims in the master plan by extracting a package and by running qemu-img create -o help; both looked obviously true until they were checked.
