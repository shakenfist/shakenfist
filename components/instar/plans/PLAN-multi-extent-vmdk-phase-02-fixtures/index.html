    Multi-extent VMDK phase 2: fixtures and baselines¶

    Status: Complete¶

    Master plan: PLAN-multi-extent-vmdk.md.

    Planning effort¶

    Medium. The phase follows a pattern PLAN-bat-population-phase-02-fixtures.md established, and it writes no instar behaviour. What raised it above mechanical is that the corpus already contains more multi-extent VMDK than the master plan's intent assumes, and that one of the two obvious fixture shapes cannot be stored at all -- both settled below by measurement rather than left to the implementer.

    Decisions already taken by the operator¶

    From the master plan, resolved 2026-11-16:

    1. Sequential extent rotation, not simultaneous indexed output devices.
    2. The read-side extent ceiling is fixed inside this plan, as phase 3.

    Neither constrains this phase directly, but decision 2 is why a fixture past the 15-extent ceiling is in scope here: phase 3 needs something to prove itself against.

    Open questions 3 to 6 remain open. This phase settles none of them; it does not touch extent path derivation (question 4) or any instar-side limit (question 5), because it writes no instar behaviour.

    Scope¶

    In scope:

    • Four qemu-written twoGbMaxExtentSparse fixtures in instar-testdata/custom/format-coverage/, with their tests/manifest.json entries.
    • A generator script for them in instar's scripts/, pinned to a static qemu-img build rather than the host's.
    • A test-time generator for twoGbMaxExtentFlat split images, which are measured below to be unstorable.
    • Baselines for the four new fixtures across all 80 static qemu builds, for every image-driven command, with the derived profiles validated.
    • All three info divergences on multi-file VMDK, two found by the survey and the third while fixing them: the top-level allocation figure and the order of children[], in the VMM, with the test helper that masks the first of them, and the fixture un-flagged that lets a multi-extent flat image be compared at all. An issue records them, and the measurements go into the master plan.

    Out of scope:

    • Reading twoGbMaxExtent* images (phase 3) and the structural comparison arm of the differential fuzzer (phase 16).
    • Baselines for the three VMDK fixtures that stay skip_qemu_img. vmdk-twogb-flat and vmdk-multi-extent name extents that deliberately do not exist, so the only baseline they could have is qemu's error text, and error-text parity is its own subject; vmdk-flat-parent and vmdk-flat-with-parent are a parentFileNameHint chain, which is neither multi-extent nor this plan's. Decision 7 records the reason against each.
    • instar map on flat sources, refused for every flat source and wider than this plan.

    What the survey found¶

    The master plan's phase 2 intent is stale in three ways, two of them load-bearing. All three are corrected at source in the master plan and docs/plans/index.md as part of this phase's planning commit; a later step should not redo that.

    The past-the-ceiling fixture already exists¶

    The intent says to add "at least one image past the current 15-extent ceiling so phase 3 has something to prove itself against". The corpus already has one: osboxes-vmdk-split-sparse (downloaded/osboxes/android-x86-5.1-rc1-split-sparse/), a real VMware Workstation twoGbMaxExtentSparse image of 26 extents, 100 GiB virtual in 1.3 GiB, with full baselines -- 261 files each under expected-outputs/qemu-img-human and qemu-img-json, 243 each under check-human, check-json and map-human, 249 under map-json, and 486 each under measure-human and measure-json.

    It is not a parity fixture, though, and the reason is worth recording: its extents are 8304640 sectors (3.96 GiB) each, not 2 GiB, under a descriptor that says createType="twoGbMaxExtentSparse". The last is 2099200 sectors. So the create type does not imply an extent size, and a reader that infers 2 GiB from the create type rather than reading each extent's declared sector count is wrong on the one real-world image in the corpus. That descriptor also carries encoding="windows-1252", isNativeSnapshot="no", and extent filenames containing spaces.

    It therefore covers the device-slot ceiling and the awkward real-world spelling, and covers qemu's own geometry not at all.

    No qemu-written split fixture exists, and the flat one cannot¶

    The intent reads as though both subformats can be added the same way. Measured, they cannot.

    A split-sparse extent is a small file: pinned case vmdk-multi-sparse-21G is 11 extents totalling 3,473,408 bytes (327,680 for each full extent, 196,608 for the 1 GiB tail). A split-flat extent is a full-length sparse file: vmdk-multi-flat-21G totals 22,548,578,304 bytes apparent.

    Sparse files do not survive git-LFS. Measured on downloaded/qemu-iotests/d2v-zerofilled.vhd: 264,308,224 bytes apparent, 512 KiB allocated in the checkout, and its LFS object at .git/lfs/objects/af/17/af17... occupies 264,314,880 bytes. The repository is already 32 GB with a 15 GB .git.

    scripts/create-vmdk-flat-testdata.sh:100-110 already works around this: it creates a 3 GiB twoGbMaxExtentFlat with qemu-img and then deletes both extents, keeping the descriptor alone (vmdk-twogb-flat.vmdk, and the manifest says so). The small vmdk-multi-flat beside it is hand-built -- three 1024-sector extents filled 0xAA/0xBB/0xCC -- so neither is qemu-written and neither has a partial last extent at qemu's geometry.

    The pin's structural oracle is sparse-only, by design¶

    tests/helpers/qemu_pins.py:711 captures structure only when a descriptor declares a SPARSE extent, with the reason in the comment: a sparse extent has a header whose fields decide how it is walked and nothing in the descriptor says what they are. So every vmdk-multi-flat-* pin case records descriptor text, extent names and extent lengths with structure: null. That is not a gap to fill; it is noted here so a later phase does not read the nulls as a defect.

    Two shipping info divergences, invisible today¶

    Found while checking what instar does with the existing fixtures. Measured with qemu-img 10.2.0 from the static builds and the instar binary at src/target/release/instar dated 2026-10-19, which predates several merges -- step 2b re-measures with a fresh build before the issue is filed.

    On custom/format-coverage/vmdk-multi-flat.vmdk (three 512 KiB fully-written flat extents):

    Field qemu-img 10.2.0 instar
    top-level actual-size 1576960 4096
    each child's actual-size 524288, 524288, 524288, 4096 same
    children[] order f003, f002, f001, descriptor f001, f002, f003, descriptor

    qemu reports the sum over the descriptor and every extent; instar reports the descriptor's allocation alone. The same holds for monolithicFlat: on vmdk-flat-1m.vmdk qemu says disk size: 1 MiB and instar says disk size: 4 KiB. In the human format instar additionally prints the descriptor's 4 KiB for every extent, where qemu prints each extent's own.

    Both are invisible because every multi-file VMDK fixture carries skip_qemu_img, and generate-baselines.py:1043 never generates for those -- 31 of the 124 safe images are flagged. And they would stay invisible after the flag came off, because tests/helpers/comparators.py:139-168 substitutes a live get_disk_size() into the baseline per dict that carries a filename: the top-level dict's filename is the descriptor, so qemu's 1576960 would be rewritten to 4096 and instar's wrong answer would match. The masking is single-file by construction, which is exactly the shape of the defect it hides.

    What was right¶

    The rest of the intent holds. instar-testdata does take direct commits to main and is git-LFS backed. generate-baselines.py has --image and --case exact-match filters that refuse to match nothing (added by PLAN-bat-population-phase-10-matrix.md), --no-commit exists and the script auto-commits without it, and scripts/validate-profiles.py exists behind make validate-profiles. The derived profile directories have twice been silently wrong while the raw baselines were fine.

    Measurements this phase starts from¶

    Taken 2026-11-18 against ca4bfb52 with the static qemu-img-binaries/x86_64/10.2.0/qemu-img, in the session scratchpad.

    Create geometry, 5 GiB sparse. Three extents of 4194304, 4194304 and 2097152 sectors; files 327,680, 327,680 and 196,608 bytes apparent; 12,288 bytes allocated each on ext4, with a 417-byte descriptor. qemu-img info reports disk size: 40 KiB, the sum of the four files' allocations. This matches pinned case vmdk-multi-sparse-5G exactly.

    Data across an extent boundary is reachable with qemu-img alone. The static builds ship qemu-img and no qemu-io, so data cannot be written into a sparse extent after the fact. Converting from a holey raw source does it in one step: a 5 GiB raw file with 128 KiB of 0xAB written at 2 GiB − 64 KiB, converted with qemu-img convert -f raw -O vmdk -o subformat=twoGbMaxExtentSparse, gives the identical three-extent descriptor and grows both of the first two extents -- 393,216 bytes apparent and 81,920 / 77,824 allocated -- so the pattern genuinely straddles the boundary. qemu-img info reports disk size: 172 KiB. Note that convert writes a 512-byte descriptor where create writes 417.

    children[] appears at 8.0.0 and its order never moves. Swept over all 80 static builds with info --output json on custom/format-coverage/vmdk-multi-flat.vmdk: 6.0.0 to 7.2.22 (30 builds) emit no children key at all, and 8.0.0 to 10.2.0 (50 builds) emit extents.2, extents.1, extents.0, file -- the extents reversed, the descriptor last. Spot-checked on monolithicFlat vmdk-flat-1m, which behaves the same way: absent at 6.0.0 and 7.2.0, extents.0, file at 8.0.0 and 10.2.0. So the reversal is parity rather than an accident of one build, and instar's own v.major >= 8 threshold for include_child_node already matches the boundary exactly.

    Allocation is not reproducible through LFS, and must not be fixed. qemu's sparse extents are hole-bearing (327,680 apparent against 12,288 allocated), so a fresh clone reports a different disk size than the machine that generated the fixture. resparsify_images() (scripts/generate-baselines.py:925) repairs that, but only under downloaded/. Extending it to custom/ would be wrong: it runs cp --sparse=always, which punches holes wherever it finds zeroes, so it would re-sparsify the deliberately dense hand-built fixtures -- vmdk-flat-1m-flat.vmdk is a megabyte of zeroes -- and churn their baselines. Nothing needs to change, because substitute_actual_size recomputes per file at test time. This paragraph exists so that nobody later reads the mismatch as a bug in resparsify_images.

    Decisions¶

    1. Split-sparse fixtures are stored; split-flat fixtures are never stored. The cost asymmetry above is three orders of magnitude, and git-LFS objects on main are effectively permanent. The existing generator's delete-the-extents trick is the precedent, and this phase makes the rule explicit rather than leaving the next person to rediscover it.

    2. Four fixtures, all twoGbMaxExtentSparse, in custom/format-coverage/:

    id Size Extents Tail Apparent cost Why
    vmdk-split-sparse-2049m 2049M 2 2048 sectors (1 MiB) 393,216 B The smallest non-multiple: a tail extent of one grain-and-a-bit
    vmdk-split-sparse-5g 5G 3 2097152 sectors (1 GiB) 851,968 B The reference geometry, identical to pinned vmdk-multi-sparse-5G
    vmdk-split-sparse-5g-boundary 5G 3 2097152 sectors 983,040 B 128 KiB of 0xAB straddling the 2 GiB boundary, so a rotation bug shows as wrong bytes rather than a wrong size

    About 7.5 MB in all, which LFS stores in full. The 33 GiB one is the phase 3 fixture; osboxes-vmdk-split-sparse is kept as the real-world foil, not replaced.

    1. The generator is pinned to the static 10.2.0 build, overridable with QEMU_IMG, and fails loudly if the binary is absent -- unlike the existing VMDK generators, which call whatever qemu-img is on the host. The honest limit of that pin: the descriptor's CID is random, so a regeneration is never byte-identical to what is committed whatever build runs it. Pinning removes the host from the inputs for everything else (ddb.toolsVersion appears from 6.2.0, per the phase 1 pin), and the fixtures are generated once and committed, not regenerated casually.

    2. The new entries do not set skip_qemu_img, so baselines generate, and each carries instar_unsupported with the refusal measured in step 2b. test_info_safe.py:124 turns that key into an assertion that instar fails, so the suite stays green today and phase 3 removes the key as part of lifting the ceiling. That is the handoff: four fixtures whose manifest entries phase 3 must change to claim success.

    3. Split-flat coverage is a test-time generator, not a fixture. A helper in tests/helpers/ builds a split-flat image into a caller's temporary directory with the pinned qemu-img, for phases 3, 11 and 16 to use. A 33 GiB split-flat image costs 17 files of 2 GiB apparent and about 4 KiB each allocated, which is free on a sparse filesystem and unstorable in LFS.

    4. Both info divergences are fixed here. The plan as first written filed them and left them to phase 3, on the grounds that the fix changes info output for every monolithicFlat image instar has ever read and cannot be proved without also changing the comparator. The operator overrode that in the back brief on 2026-11-18: fix them in this phase. So the phase gains three changes it did not have.

    The VMM reports the top-level allocation as the sum over the descriptor and every resolved extent, and the human path stats each extent instead of reusing the input file's block count. Both halves were re-measured on a binary built 2026-11-18 and both still reproduce: on vmdk-multi-flat qemu reports 1576960 against instar's 4096, and on monolithicFlat vmdk-flat-1m 1052672 against 4096. The JSON children path already stats each extent correctly (src/vmm/src/main.rs:2768), so the defect is the top-level figure and the human rendering, not the per-child JSON.

    children[] is emitted in qemu's order, and only where qemu emits the array at all. This was measured across all 80 builds before anything copied it, because a reversal that moved between versions would be a divergence to document rather than match. It does not move: no build from 6.0.0 to 7.2.22 emits children for a multi-file VMDK, every build from 8.0.0 to 10.2.0 emits it with the extents reversed and the descriptor last, and that holds for monolithicFlat as well as the split subformat. instar's include_child_node is already v.major >= 8 (src/vmm/src/version.rs:123), so it has the boundary right and only the order wrong.

    What it does not have right is the override beside it: if profile.include_child_node || vmdk_flat.is_some() (src/vmm/src/main.rs:2763, added 2026-05-15 in c284872c to emit per-extent children for monolithicFlat) bypasses the version gate, so instar info --qemu-version 7.2 emits a children array where every qemu build before 8.0.0 emits none. That is the third divergence, found while checking the second, and it is fixed in the same step.

    tests/helpers/comparators.py sums the siblings for the top-level figure of a multi-file image, so the oracle stops substituting the descriptor's own allocation over qemu's sum. This is what makes the first fix provable, and it is why the fix and the helper change land together: either alone turns a green suite red.

    All three are filed as #922, which the management session wrote from step 2b's measurements and the 80-build sweep, so the record of the defect exists independently of the commit that fixes it.

    What makes this affordable is something the survey found late. vmdk-flat-1m and vmdk-flat-10m do not carry skip_qemu_img -- they run in CI and already have info baselines -- so the monolithicFlat half of the fix is provable against baselines that already exist, with no generation step in between. That is also the answer to how a shipping divergence on a CI fixture went unnoticed: the masking, not a missing fixture.

It is the one complete multi-extent flat image in the corpus -- three 512 KiB extents, all present -- so it is the only fixture that can compare a multi-extent flat info against qemu's, which is half of what decision 6 fixes. The three that keep the flag keep it for stated reasons: vmdk-twogb-flat and vmdk-multi-extent name extents that deliberately do not exist, and vmdk-flat-parent with vmdk-flat-with-parent form a parentFileNameHint chain that is not multi-extent. The testdata half lands as a direct commit to main, which the back brief gates.

    Risks and mitigations¶

    • An LFS object pushed to main is permanent. 7.5 MB is small, but a wrong fixture cannot be cheaply unpublished. Mitigation: the back brief gates the fixture set and the push separately, and the management session checks each file's apparent size against the table in decision 2 before anything is added.
    • The baseline generator auto-commits. Without --no-commit it commits to main by itself. Mitigation: stated in step 2e's brief as not optional; the management session checks git log in the testdata clone before reviewing the diff.
    • The derived profiles have twice been silently wrong while the raw baselines were fine. Mitigation: step 2e runs make validate-profiles and additionally opens one profile by hand; the management session re-runs the validator itself.
    • A fixture that quietly joins the #845 cohort. 31 of 124 safe images carry skip_qemu_img and generate nothing. Mitigation: decision 4 forbids the flag, and the definition of done counts the files that appear.
    • The 2 GiB-extent assumption. The one real-world split-sparse image in the corpus has 3.96 GiB extents under a twoGbMaxExtentSparse create type. Mitigation: recorded in the survey above and carried into the master plan, so phase 3 reads declared sector counts rather than inferring from the create type.
    • An info output change has a wide blast radius. Decision 6's fix changes the allocation figure instar prints for every multi-file VMDK it has ever read, which is monolithicFlat as well as the split subformats. Mitigation: step 2d runs make test as well as the python suite, because the VMM's own unit tests live in the file it edits; it mutation-checks each of the four changes rather than reading the guards; and the management session re-runs the monolithicFlat comparison against the baselines that already exist, which is the one case provable without any generation step.
    • Copying a divergence that is not stable. Matching qemu's children[] order is only parity if qemu's order is the same across builds. Answered before step 2d was briefed: swept over all 80 builds, the array is absent below 8.0.0 and identically reversed on all 50 builds from 8.0.0 up. The risk was real and is now closed, so step 2d matches the order rather than documenting a divergence.
    • The comparator change and the VMM change must land together. Either alone turns a green suite red: the helper would reject instar's current output, and the VMM fix would be substituted away. Mitigation: both are one step and one commit, and the step's brief says so.
    • Measuring instar with a stale binary. The divergence table was taken with a binary dated 2026-10-19. Mitigation: step 2b rebuilds and re-measures before anything is filed, and steps 2d and 2g use 2b's numbers rather than the plan's.

    Removing the vmdk_flat.is_some() override was the change most worth checking, since it narrows output. git show c284872c states the premise it was added on -- that qemu-img emitted children for monolithicFlat "back to 6.0" -- which is exactly the claim the 80-build sweep disproved, and no baseline below 8.0.0 carries a children array at any profile. The override was the bug and version.rs was right.

    make test reported five failures, all of them the known contention cluster on this host: the four test_convert VHD cases exit 4, which is COMPARE_EXIT_READ_ERROR rather than the exit code for a content difference, and the test_dd case reports a guest info operation timing out. The host was at a load average near 20 on 16 cores. All five pass on replay at concurrency 2. That the convert helper reports a read error as a content difference, and discards the stderr that would say so, is recorded as future work.

    Steps 2f and 2g, 2026-11-18 (AEDT). Both ran as planned and both needed review corrections, none of them large enough to send back.

    In 2f the generator looked its build up with shutil.which, which searches PATH for a bare name and would quietly resolve to the host's qemu-img -- the fallback every other part of this work refuses.
