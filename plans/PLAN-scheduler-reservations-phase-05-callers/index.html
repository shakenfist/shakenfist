    Scheduler reservations phase 5 — caller consolidation and the hard ceiling¶

    Prompt¶

    Before responding to questions or discussion points in this document, explore the shakenfist codebase thoroughly. Read relevant source files, understand existing patterns (object lifecycle, state machines, MariaDB storage via the three-layer direct/gRPC/public pattern, Pydantic schemas, daemon architecture, operation queue system, event logging), and ground your answers in what the code actually does today. Do not speculate about the codebase when you could read it instead. Where a question touches on external concepts (KVM/libvirt, VXLAN networking, MariaDB/Galera, gRPC/protobuf), research as needed to give a confident answer. Flag any uncertainty explicitly rather than guessing.

    Consult ARCHITECTURE.md, AGENTS.md and GOALS.md. Key references: shakenfist/scheduler.py (the Scheduler class and find_candidates()), shakenfist/instance.py (place_instance() and the advisory claim accounting), shakenfist/external_api/instance.py and shakenfist/operations/node_inst_netdesc_op.py (the two placement-walk call sites), and shakenfist/mariadb.py (admit_instance_placement() and CLAIM_ENFORCEMENT_HARD).

    Planning effort¶

    High. This phase turns on correctness: a shared admission helper that two callers must use identically, and a flip from advisory to hard ceiling enforcement whose edge cases (ground-truth writers, the demand re-walk, transient-vs-permanent refusals) are easy to get subtly wrong. Review at high effort for the same reason.

    Scope¶

    In scope:

    1. Extract the duplicated placement walk (the place_walk closure plus the demand-waived re-walk) that exists verbatim in external_api/instance.py and operations/node_inst_netdesc_op.py into one shared helper, and route both callers through it. The in-code comments in both files name this as phase 5's job and cite this plan.
    2. Remove admin.py's Scheduler() construction so the capacity view is a plain read of the published capacity surface, not the construction of a placement decider. Phase 7a deliberately left this for phase 5 (7a's H7).
    3. (Gated on the calibration re-run — see Decision D5.) Flip CLAIM_ENFORCEMENT_HARD from advisory to hard: turn the advisory over-claim event into a refusal (HTTP 403 with the structured body D16 specifies — claim uuid, per-dimension limit, current drawdown, shortfall), and split claim denials out of TRANSIENT_CAPACITY_STAGES so a namespace-quota refusal is not advertised as retryable.
    4. Correct the master plan's phase 5 stub and the D11/D16 framing at source (done in this planning commit — see What the survey found), and document the retained in-Python pre-filters.

    Out of scope:

    • Removing the in-Python capacity filters. The stub says to; the survey shows they are not legacy (Decision D1). They stay.
    • A new read-only feasibility precheck in instance.py. D11 imagined one; the create path already claims synchronously, and a second decider is the disease this plan cures (Decision D2).
    • The ranking model. cpu_load_1-vs-occupancy ordering is phase 6's, already closed.
    • The push audit. Phase 9, last.

    What the survey found¶

    The phase 5 stub (PLAN-scheduler-reservations.md:847-902) and decision D11 were written in 2026-07 and are substantially stale; the staleness changes the phase's shape, so it is recorded here and corrected at source in the planning commit.

    F1 — instance.py is not a "read-only feasibility precheck"; it is a real claim-consuming admission point. D11 says "external_api/instance.py keeps a read-only feasibility precheck … claim nothing." In fact the create path at external_api/instance.py:1125-1200 calls find_candidates() and then walks the candidates calling inst.place_instance(), whose guarded transaction (instance.py:1285) is the admission. The comment at external_api/instance.py:1163-1182 states this outright: "the guarded capacity claim inside place_instance() is what actually admits the instance." So two of the three callers already consume claims; the migration D11 imagined has in effect already happened.

    F2 — the real caller work is de-duplication, not migration. The placement walk (a place_walk(enforce_demand) closure, then a demand-waived second walk) exists verbatim in both operations/node_inst_netdesc_op.py:249-290 and the create path in external_api/instance.py. Both carry the identical comment: "until a shared helper is extracted (see PLAN-scheduler-reservations.md), a semantic change here must be made there too." That duplication, not a caller still on an old path, is what phase 5 removes.

    F3 — the in-Python capacity filters are not legacy. _has_sufficient_cpu/_ram/_disk (scheduler.py:445-636) were re-engineered on 2026-08-14 into ledger-aware pre-filters that read the same scheduler_node_capacity counters the guard draws down, charging each node max(measured, committed). Their docstring (scheduler.py:446-473) is explicit: "This is not the admission decision. The real guard is the atomic UPDATE … This filter's job is to prune the candidate list cheaply so that the guard misses less often." Removing them (as the stub's "remove the legacy in-Python capacity filtering" instructs) reintroduces the exact failure that re-engineering fixed: a full-but-idle-measuring node winning the load ordering and then being refused by the guard, which cost merge CI a whole suite of creates on 2026-08-14. They stay.

    F4 — admin.py's capacity view already reads the typed columns. D11 says it "becomes a read over the typed capacity columns." That already happened in phase 7a: summarize_resources() (scheduler.py:1138) reads the counters through _capacity_by_node() and _cluster_capacity() and publishes cluster_capacity and excluded (scheduler.py:1164-1200). All that remains is that admin.py:177 still does s = scheduler.Scheduler(); return s.summarize_resources() — constructing a placement decider to call one read method. Removing that construction is the only admin.py work left, and 7a's test_nodes.py tests pin the two keys the read must keep.

    F5 — the hard-ceiling scaffolding is all in place; the flip is a flag plus a refusal. CLAIM_ENFORCEMENT_HARD = False (mariadb.py:27461) is the single gate. The SQL layer already honours it: claim_guarded = enforce and CLAIM_ENFORCEMENT_HARD (mariadb.py:28635), and admit_instance_placement() already returns claim_over_limit, claim_dimensions and claim_uuid. Today instance.py:_event_claim_over_limit() (instance.py:1206-1270) fires a warning on an admitted-but-over-claim placement; its own docstring says "Phase 5 turns the same condition into a refusal." The flip therefore has three parts: set the flag, convert that advisory event into a 403 refusal carrying the structured body, and (F6) fix the transient-stage classification.

    F6 — flipping the flag has a known side effect in base.py. TRANSIENT_CAPACITY_STAGES (external_api/base.py:135-148) includes CAPACITY_GUARD_STAGE with a comment: "This holds only while mariadb.CLAIM_ENFORCEMENT_HARD is False … Once that constant is flipped … a claim denial becomes namespace quota exhaustion, which no wait clears, and claim denials must be split out of this set at that point." The flip step owns that split, or a claim refusal inherits a Retry-After that will never succeed.

    F7 — the gate is a calibration re-run, recorded as plan-blocker b13. D16's "advisory for one release, then hard" was corrected on 2026-08-27 to gate on phase 4c's observation record rather than elapsed releases, because nothing calibrates against an empty claim population. As of this planning run that record needs re-running on a populated cluster (plan-blocker b13). The flip step (step 3) must not be implemented until b13 clears.

    Nothing else in the stub was found wrong: the queue worker (node_inst_netdesc_op.py) is indeed a placement caller, and the three call sites are exactly the three the stub names.

    Decisions¶

    D1 — the in-Python capacity pre-filters stay, and the master plan is corrected to say so. This reverses the stub's "remove the legacy in-Python capacity filtering." They are not a second uncoordinated decider: since 2026-08-14 they read the same counters the guard draws down and exist only to prune the candidate list so the guard misses less often. This is the decision a reviewer is most likely to challenge, because the stub is explicit and this contradicts it. The rebuttal is in F3: the filters were rebuilt specifically to track the ledger, their own docstring disclaims being the admission decision, and removing them is a known regression with a dated merge-CI cost, not a cleanup. The plan keeps them and adds one sentence to the master plan recording why the instruction is discharged.

    D2 — no new read-only precheck is added to instance.py. D11 wanted one; F1 shows the create path already finds candidates and claims synchronously, and find_candidates()'s cheap filters already "fail obviously-unfittable requests early." Adding a separate read-only precheck now would be a second placement decider, which D11's own last sentence forbids ("two uncoordinated schedulers is the disease this plan cures").

    D3 — the shared placement helper is a function on Instance, place_on_candidates(candidates, …), in shakenfist/instance.py. Both call sites already hold an Instance and call inst.place_instance() per candidate; the walk, the denial-collection dict, the two-pass demand logic and the exhaustion outcome are the natural second half of place_instance's own contract and belong beside it. It returns the chosen node or None, and records the per-candidate denials the callers event today, so neither caller loses an event. The callers keep their own divergent before (candidate construction, forced lists) and after (HTTP response vs operation redirect) handling; only the walk is shared. The alternative — a free function in scheduler.py — was rejected because the walk mutates an Instance and reads nothing from the Scheduler.

    D4 — admin.py calls scheduler.summarize_cluster_resources(), a module-level function. summarize_resources() reads no instance state and only needs a fresh metrics/counter snapshot; it does not need a Scheduler object. Make a module-level summarize_cluster_resources() that does the metric refresh and the read, and have the bound method delegate to it (or move the body and drop the method if no other caller remains — the survey step confirms callers first). admin.py then calls the function. The two 7a keys (cluster_capacity, excluded) and 7a's test_nodes.py assertions are the regression guard.

    D5 — the ceiling flip is step 3, last, and gated on b13. Steps 1 and 2 (de-duplication, admin.py) depend on nothing external and are implementable and reviewable immediately. Step 3 (the advisory→hard flip) must not be implemented until the calibration re-run (plan-blocker b13) has produced the observation record D16 gates on.     Risks and mitigations¶

    • The shared helper diverges in behaviour from one of the two callers.     • admin.py's response body changes shape when de-Schedulered. Mitigation: step 2 keeps 7a's test_nodes.py assertions as the guard and requires the body be byte-for-byte unchanged.
