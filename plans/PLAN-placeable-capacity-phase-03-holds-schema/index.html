    Placeable capacity phase 3: shape holds, schema and the node guard¶

    Prompt¶

    Before responding to questions or discussion points in this document, explore the shakenfist codebase thoroughly and ground your answers in what the code actually does today. Do not speculate when you could read instead. Flag uncertainty explicitly.

    This is phase 3 of PLAN-placeable-capacity.md. Read that master plan's "Phase 3: shape holds — schema and the node guard (A)" section first, its G5, G6 and G8 requirements, its What a hold promises, and what it does not section, and phase 0's D7, D8, D9 and D15 (PLAN-placeable-capacity-phase-00-decisions.md). D8 is the schema decision this phase implements, and its Left to phase 3 paragraph is the list of questions this plan answers.

    Three plans number their decisions D-something, and the numbers collide. In this file, a bare Dn is a decision of this phase plan. A decision of PLAN-placeable-capacity-phase-00-decisions.md is written "phase 0's D8". A decision of shakenfist/private-ci's PLAN-placeable-claims phase 0, which the master plan calls the brief, is written "the brief's D3". The master plan itself uses the second and third of those conventions inconsistently, which is noted in F9 below and is why this paragraph exists.

    Citations below are into develop at 0b0271ef64 unless stated otherwise.

    Planning effort¶

    High, as the master plan specifies, and the review effort is high too. This phase adds a table to the capacity schema, a term to the admission guard that every instance create passes through, and a counter the reconciler must recompute rather than adjust. Each of those three has a failure mode that is silent: a migration that skips a column on an upgraded database, a guard whose arithmetic disagrees with the pre-filter in front of it, and a reconciler pass that overwrites the bookings it was meant to total. None of them fails a test that does not exist to catch it, which is why DoD items here are mostly mutations and arithmetic identities rather than feature checks.

    Scope¶

    In:

    • A namespace_claim_holds table: one row per hold, the ground truth phase 0's D8 requires.
    • held_cpus, held_memory_mb and held_disk_gb on scheduler_node_capacity, with the version bump and the existence-guarded ADD COLUMN migration that implies.
    • The booking path in the core: creating holds with a claim, choosing their nodes through phase 1's find_candidates_for_shapes(), and the sum(holds) <= limit_* invariant tested in SQL inside the claim's own transaction, at creation and at update.
    • The release paths that belong to the claim's own lifecycle: claim delete, the reconciler's expiry sweep, and a shrink that leaves the hold sum above the new limit.
    • The node guard term (G6): used + held + request <= limit in _claim_node(), and held carried in the per-dimension denial detail.
    • The reconciler's recomputation of the three counters from hold rows, in the pass that already recomputes used_*.
    • The pre-filter's agreement with that guard, and the brief's D2 narrowing of the live memory check.
    • Visibility (G8) in _summarize_resources().
    • The proto fields and the sf-database RPCs the above needs.

    Out:

    • Any API surface, and the capability token for A. See D6. Nothing a REST caller can reach creates a hold in this phase; the booking path is reachable from NamespaceClaim and from sf-database's RPC, and is exercised by tests.
    • Consumption: implicit best fit, the POST /instances hold reference, and moving a hold's room from held_* to used_*. That is phase 4, under G3 and phase 0's D7.
    • Displacement and re-placement when a node leaves service. Phase 4, under phase 0's D9. What this phase does instead with a hold whose node has gone is D8 below.
    • The refusal contract: stage/transient/Retry-After on claim refusals, and the reclassification of a held refusal as non-transient. Phase 5. R3 is the sequencing hazard that creates, and it is a hazard for phase 4 rather than for this phase.
    • sf_claim, sf-client and the operator-facing documentation of how to express a shape. Phase 6, under phase 0's D11.
    • The _derive_disk_limit_gb() defect that is not a defect: see D9. Held disk stays outside that derivation deliberately.

    What the survey found¶

    Phase 0's D7 and D8 are unusually complete for a design this phase inherits — between them they settle the hold's identity, its lifetime, its storage, the reconciler's obligation and five rejected alternatives, and D8 ends with an explicit list of what it left here. So the survey's job was mostly to re-find the citations and to check the three claims the master plan's own phase 3 section makes in its own words. Two of those three are right, one is ambiguous in a way that matters, and phase 0's D8 contains one instruction this phase declines (D4).

    F2. "The per-node held column beside used_*" is ambiguous, and only one reading is implementable. The master plan's phase 3 section and Q4 both say the schema decision is "a ledger dimension beside used_*" without saying which table's used_*. There are two: namespace_claims.used_* (mariadb.py:4268-4276) and scheduler_node_capacity.used_* (:4194-4202). A hold books one shape onto one node, so a per-node quantity cannot live on the claim row — and _direct_admit_instance_placement() says in terms why not: a claim's counters "are namespace-denominated and node-independent" (:29688-29693). Phase 0's D8 resolves this correctly and in the only direction that works: the counters go on scheduler_node_capacity, the per-(claim, node, shape) detail goes in a new table, and there is no held column on namespace_claims or on cluster_capacity. This phase follows D8. The master plan's wording is corrected at its source in the planning commit, because a reader who takes the other reading designs a ledger that cannot be guarded in SQL.

    F3. Nothing of this phase's scope has shipped. grep -rln 'shape_hold\|claim_hold\|held_cpus\|held_memory\|namespace_claim_holds' over *.py, *.proto and the repository's SQL returns nothing. Unlike phases 1 and 2, whose premises went stale because other work arrived first, this phase's premise is intact: no table, no column, no proto field, no helper.

    F4. "Holds sit inside claimed" is true, and the reconciler is where it is true. The master plan's phase 3 section rests the claim-branch argument on it, so it was checked rather than taken: cluster_capacity.claimed_* is the sum of limits of claims in coverage state active, accumulated at mariadb.py:28177-28183 and written at :28228-28230. A hold lives inside its claim's limit_*, which is therefore already inside claimed_*. So no cluster-side counter is needed, the claim branch of the admission transaction is genuinely unchanged, and _claim_capacity_guard() must not gain a hold term — which is also what its own docstring warns twice about, for the adjacent migrated term (:30711-30725).

    F5. The node guard exists twice, and the second copy does not cover disk. The guard phase 3 must change is the WHERE clause in _claim_node() (mariadb.py:29769-29793), which tests all three dimensions. In front of it sits the scheduler's pre-filter, which charges the same ledger so that a burst does not get attracted to a node that will then refuse it (issue 3636): _has_sufficient_cpu() reads used_cpus against limit_cpus (scheduler.py:1052-1065) and _has_sufficient_ram() reads used_memory_mb against limit_memory_mb (:1130-1159). _has_sufficient_disk() reads no capacity row at all (:1162-1184): it tests filesystem free space minus the node's reservation, and the disk ledger appears nowhere in the pre-filter. So after this phase a node's held disk is invisible until admission. That is not a new asymmetry — used_disk_gb is equally invisible there today — and place_on_candidates() walks to the next candidate on a guard denial rather than failing the create (instance.py:1628-1645), so the cost is a wasted admission attempt per candidate, not a spurious refusal. D11 keeps the asymmetry rather than closing it, and says why that is not laziness.

    F6. disk_available has no ledger term, which is why phase 0's D8's visibility instruction cannot be followed literally. D8 says _summarize_resources() should net held room out of cpu_available, ram_available and disk_available. The first two already carry a ledger term: cpu_available is hard_max_cpus - max(measured, committed) (scheduler.py:817) and ram_available is explicitly min()-ed against limit_memory_mb - committed_mb (:846-852). disk_available is disk_free_instances / GiB - disk_reservation_gb (:857-864) — a filesystem measurement with no ledger in it at all, which is why the capacity row's disk counters are published beside it rather than folded into it, for the reason the comment at :865-874 gives (headroom moves under load, the ledger ceiling does not; issue 4208). Netting held disk out of it would be the first ledger term in a measured figure, and it would disagree with the guard, which tests against limit_disk_gb. D4 amends D8 on this one point and leaves the rest of it intact.

    F7. The _derive_disk_limit_gb() trap D8 warns about is real and concrete. The limit is used_disk_gb + headroom (mariadb.py:27365-27400), and the reconciler passes the freshly recomputed used[2] into it (:28104-28108). If held disk ever entered that used term, the guard used + held + request <= limit would expand to request <= headroom and admit every hold a second time. The call site is one line and the mistake is one word, so D9 pins it with a mutation rather than a comment.

    Decisions¶

    D1. The table is namespace_claim_holds, with no foreign keys, server-side timestamps, and three indexes.

    namespace_claim_holds
  uuid          CHAR(32) PRIMARY KEY     -- uuid_column_type()
  claim_uuid    CHAR(32) NOT NULL
  namespace     VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL
  cpus          BIGINT NOT NULL
  memory_mb     BIGINT NOT NULL
  disk_gb       BIGINT NOT NULL
  node_uuid     CHAR(32) NULL            -- NULL means displaced
  instance_uuid CHAR(32) NULL            -- the consuming instance
  created_at    DATETIME(6) NOT NULL     -- see the execution notes
  updated_at    DATETIME(6) NOT NULL
  INDEX idx_namespace_claim_holds_claim (claim_uuid)
  INDEX idx_namespace_claim_holds_node  (node_uuid)
  INDEX idx_namespace_claim_holds_instance (instance_uuid)

    The dimension columns are named cpus/memory_mb/disk_gb and not held_*: on a hold row the whole row is the holding, so a held_ prefix would be redundant where on the node row it is the distinction that matters. BIGINT throughout, for the reason the other three capacity tables give (mariadb.py:4186-4190): the proto fields are int64 and the singleton sums them.

    namespace is carried even though it is derivable from the claim, because the per-node fold and the release paths both filter on it and a join to namespace_claims on the admission hot path is what phase 0's D8 built the counters to avoid. It is a denormalisation and the reconciler is its authority, like every other counter in this family.

    Server-side DATETIME rather than object_references' float epochs, because this table is a member of the capacity family and follows that family's cluster_locks TIMESTAMP idiom: created_at orders a shrink's releases (D5) and updated_at is compared against the database's NOW(), never a per-node clock. Amended during step 3d: both are DATETIME(6), because at whole-second precision the ordering created_at exists to provide was decided by the uuid tie-break whenever two bookings landed in the same second. The execution notes carry the three ways that change could have been made without taking effect. Three indexes because there are exactly three access paths: the sum invariant filters by claim, the per-node fold groups by node, and phase 4's consumption and release look up by instance.

    No foreign keys, per F8.

    D2. Consumption state is the nullable instance_uuid alone. Phase 0's D8 left open "whether the consumption state is its own column or is read from the nullable instance uuid alone". It is read from the uuid. A separate boolean would be a second ledger that agrees with the first only until the next write — the reason _summarize_resources() publishes the singleton rather than recomputing it (scheduler.py:676-690) — and it would buy nothing, because D8's residue formula needs the consuming instance's request and so needs the uuid regardless. The cost is that "consumed" and "consumed by an instance that no longer exists" are the same state until the reconciler notices; that is phase 4's release path, and until phase 4 ships nothing sets the column.

    D3. The holds table is last in the canonical table order, and the sum invariant is a scalar subquery inside the guarded UPDATE, never a separate SELECT.

    Phase 0's D7 left this phase "where the hold-taking statement sits in the transaction's canonical table order". The order today is cluster_capacity, then namespace_claims, then scheduler_node_capacity (mariadb.py:29513-29520), and it is what keeps concurrent admissions from deadlocking rather than merely recovering. namespace_claim_holds goes after all three. That position changes no existing writer's order, so the deadlock-freedom argument carries over unchanged, and it is the order both transactions that touch holds want anyway: booking tests the claim and increments the node before writing the hold row, and phase 4's consumption excludes the hold from the node guard and then marks the row.

    The sum invariant is the subtle half. sum(holds) <= limit_* has to be tested inside the claim's transaction, and that transaction's first statement must be a guarded UPDATE or every writer that loses a race dies of ER_CHECKREAD (1020) under innodb_snapshot_isolation, which is on by default from MariaDB 11.6.2 — the invariant stated at mariadb.py:29662-29675 and measured at 46 of 50 concurrent admissions exhausting their retry budget. A plain SELECT SUM(...) FROM namespace_claim_holds added to the claim transaction is exactly the statement that invariant forbids, and it reads a table this transaction holds no locks on, so it would be the one read that cannot be excused as "after our own writes". The invariant therefore rides as a correlated scalar subquery in the WHERE of the claim's own guarded UPDATE:

    UPDATE namespace_claims SET ... WHERE uuid = :claim
  AND :new_limit_cpus >= COALESCE(
      (SELECT SUM(cpus) FROM namespace_claim_holds
        WHERE claim_uuid = :claim), 0)
  AND ...

    Phase 0's D8 already said this is affordable — claim create and update are not a hot path, unlike admission — and the subquery form is what makes it safe as well as affordable. COALESCE is load bearing: SUM over no rows is NULL, and x >= NULL is NULL, which is not true, so a claim with no holds would fail its own guard.

    D4. Held room is netted out of cpu_available and ram_available and is not netted out of disk_available. This amends phase 0's D8. F6 has the reasoning: the first two figures already carry a ledger term and are already min()-ed against the ledger ceiling, so netting held out of them keeps them saying what they say today. disk_available is a filesystem measurement, and netting a booking out of it would be the first ledger term in it, would disagree with the guard, and would contradict the comment that explains why the disk ledger is published beside it rather than folded into it. All three *_held figures are published either way, so nothing is hidden; what changes is which published figure already accounts for them. A client-side ledger model that reads disk_available and wants the ledger reads disk_limit_gb, disk_committed_gb and disk_held_gb, exactly as it must today to see committed disk.

    This is the decision in this plan most likely to be argued with, because it declines an instruction from a landed design phase. The narrow case for following D8 literally is that G8's stated purpose is that summarize_resources() "must net holds out, or every client-side ledger model counts booked room as free" — and on disk, it will. The answer is that it already counts committed room as free there, that the figure is not a ledger and cannot be made into one by subtraction, and that the private-ci consumer phase 2 was built for reads the per-node figures rather than the aggregate (PLAN-placeable-claims-phase-05a-fit-by-query.md). The amendment is recorded at D8's own site in the planning commit so the next reader meets it there.

    D5. Field names follow their neighbours on both sides, which means the column and the published figure differ. The columns are held_cpus, held_memory_mb, held_disk_gb, beside used_cpus/used_memory_mb/used_disk_gb. The published figures are cpu_held, ram_held_mb, disk_held_gb, beside cpu_committed, ram_committed, disk_committed_gb. Phase 0's D8 wrote the middle one as memory_held_mb; that would match the column and clash with every figure around it, and this codebase already carries exactly this split — the column is used_memory_mb and the figure it feeds is ram_committed (scheduler.py:845). Consistency with the immediate neighbours wins on each side of the boundary.

    D6. This phase ships no API surface and advertises no capability. G5 requires one capability string for A, and a capability token is a promise that the thing works. A does not work until a create can consume a hold, which is phase 4. So advertising the token here would advertise a half-built A, and shipping a booking endpoint without a token is the other thing G5 forbids ("additive and advertised"). Phase 4 adds the endpoint and the token together. This phase's booking path is the sf-database RPC and a NamespaceClaim method, exercised by unit and live tests.

    The consequence is deliberate and is the mitigation for R3: held_* is zero in every production cluster for this phase's release, because nothing a caller can reach writes a hold row. Every arithmetic change this phase makes is therefore a no-op in production until phase 4, which is the right shape for a change to the guard every instance create passes through.

    The argument against is that a phase which ships a schema, a guard term and a reconciler fold with no reachable producer ships dead code for a release. That is true, and it is the better of the two failure modes: the alternative ships a reachable producer of rows that nothing can consume, release on delete, or displace, in a release whose refusal contract still tells callers that a hold refusal is transient.

    D7. The pre-filter gains the held term in its ledger sub-checks for cpu and memory, and the live memory sub-check gains the brief's D2 narrowing. Three changes in scheduler.py:

    • _has_sufficient_cpu(): the ledger side becomes committed + held, so current_cpu = max(measured_cpus, committed_cpus + held_cpus). The self-exclusion for a reschedule onto the same node applies to committed only, because a hold is not the instance being replaced.
    • _has_sufficient_ram(), third sub-check: the same, against limit_memory_mb.
    • _has_sufficient_ram(), first sub-check: available is reduced by the node's unconsumed held memory. This is the brief's D2 first narrowing — "every other create's live check counts a node's unconsumed holds as if resident" — and it is the one place in this phase where a ledger quantity deliberately enters a measured figure. It is justified where D4's disk netting is not: a booked instance's memory will become resident, and the live check exists to predict the OOM kills that set RAM_OVERCOMMIT_RATIO to 1.0, so a booking is exactly the kind of future residency it should fear. Filesystem disk has no equivalent — a booked disk consumes no blocks until something writes to it.

    Why the pre-filter at all, when the guard is the decision: because a pre-filter that disagrees with the guard attracts a burst to a node that then refuses all of it, which is issue 3636 and is the reason the ledger terms are in these filters today (scheduler.py:1130-1140).

    D8. A hold whose node has no capacity row contributes to no node's held_*, and this phase does not displace it. The reconciler deletes capacity rows for nodes that have left service (mariadb.py:28167-28170), and the held_* fold is restricted to nodes that still hold a row after the pass — the same restriction the unclaimed fold already uses, for the reason given at :28201-28214 (an instance stranded on a drained hypervisor must inflate neither side of a total its node no longer contributes to). The hold row keeps pointing at the departed node until phase 4 displaces it. With no foreign keys (F8) that dangles nothing, and phase 0's D9 owns the policy.

    D9. held_disk_gb never enters _derive_disk_limit_gb(), and a mutation proves a test says so. F7 is the trap. A comment is not enough for a one-word mistake on a line that already takes a used_* value as its first argument, so step 3i mutates used[2] to used[2] + held[2] at mariadb.py:28104 and the phase is not done until a named test fails on it.

    D10. The reconciler recomputes held_* as SUM(size - consumed_request) over the node's hold rows whose claim is active, in the pass and the read that already recompute used_*. Phase 0's D8 fixes this; what is left to decide is where the consuming instance's request comes from. It comes from the instances row the existing usage SQL already joins (_RECONCILE_USAGE_SQL, mariadb.py:27587), not from a column on the hold row: a copy of the request on the hold row is a third ledger, and the instance row is the same ground truth used_* is computed from, so the residue and the usage cannot disagree about one instance. An unconsumed hold's residue is its full size, which falls out of the same expression with a LEFT JOIN.

    D11. The disk pre-filter asymmetry stays. F5: adding a ledger disk term to _has_sufficient_disk() would be a behaviour change for every create in the cluster, held or not, on a stage whose refusals are the largest single family in the private-ci reading phase 5a just took. It is out of this phase's scope, it is not required by G6 (which is about the guard), and it should be decided with the measurement rather than on the way past. It is recorded in the master plan's Future work in the planning commit.

    Step 3f, the reconciler fold¶

    The R5 warning named the wrong mechanism, and the step was right to ignore it. The 3f row told the step that the hold-side join "needs the same REPLACE treatment" the object_references and object_states joins carry. It does not. uuid_column_type() is sa.Uuid(native_uuid=False), which is the undashed CHAR(32) form -- the same form instances.uuid stores -- so h.instance_uuid = i.uuid compares like with like and a REPLACE there would have been a no-op on the data and misleading as documentation, pointing a later reader at a conversion that is not happening. Only the two reference tables store the dashed 36-character form. R5's failure mode is untouched by this: a join that matches nothing still reports zero held and overwrites the counters, and the step proved its test bites by mutating the claim join to match nothing, which failed nine tests, seven of them live. The row has been corrected to ask that the form be checked rather than asserting what it is.

    The fold is clamped per hold, not per node. A hold whose consuming instance was resized larger than the hold would otherwise contribute a negative residue and quietly offset another hold's booking on the same node, so each hold's contribution is GREATEST(0, size - consumed) before the SUM. Per-node clamping would have let the offset through.

    D9's trap has a test that bites. Mutating the _derive_disk_limit_gb() call site from used[2] to used[2] + held[2] failed exactly the two limit_disk_gb_is_unchanged_by_booked_disk tests and nothing else, which is the narrow signal that the assertion is about that term rather than incidentally sensitive to it.

    Step 3g, publishing held room¶

    The step netted held in before the maximum, not after, and that is the right reading of D4. The row asked that held be netted out of cpu_available. The step instead charged the node max(measured, committed + held), which is verbatim what _has_sufficient_cpu() does, and that function's docstring says it charges "whichever of the two is larger, which is the same arithmetic summarize_resources() publishes". Step 3e taught the pre-filter about holds and left that sentence false; this form makes it true again. Subtracting after the maximum would have left the published figure and the pre-filter disagreeing on every node where the measurement binds, which is R2's concern one layer out, in the figure an operator reads rather than the one admission applies.

    Three mutations, re-run by the management session. Netting held out of disk_available fails exactly test_summarize_resources_does_not_net_held_out_of_disk; dropping held from the cpu term and from the ram term each fail the netting test and the cluster-totals test. The disk test books 40 GB of held disk on the node it asserts over, which is what makes it bite: with held disk at zero it would pass whether or not the netting existed.

    Step 3h, the documentation¶

    The step read the code rather than this plan, and caught two false claims in it. Both were this session's.

    D10 and the 3h row describe the held counters as "recomputed and never adjusted". That is not what was built. Booking increments held_* in its guarded UPDATE and release decrements it with a floor; the reconciler recomputes from the hold rows and overwrites whatever it finds. The thing that never touches held_* is admission, which charges used_*. The documentation says the latter. The recompute-not-adjust principle still holds where it matters -- the reconciler never reads a counter to derive it -- but the blanket form of the sentence was wrong and would have been copied forward.

    The execution note on the refusal reasons said none of the four was in CLAIM_REFUSAL_STATUS. not_found (404) and not_active (409) are in it, and in CLAIM_REFUSAL_MESSAGE as well. Only claim_capacity and node_capacity are new. The note has been corrected; as written it would have had phase 4 map twice the tokens it owes.

    What the review corrected in the step's own work. The holds section said a hold ends in "exactly three ways" and then described a fourth two paragraphs later: there are four callers of _release_hold_rows() -- an explicit release, claim delete, the expiry sweep, and a shrink. The count is now four, with a sentence saying all four share the helper, which is why the counters come back the same way however a hold ended. A rewrap had also left a CI docstring line ragged, and a new paragraph had run into the sentence after it.
