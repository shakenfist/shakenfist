    Phase 4: drain sfcbr and keep it drained¶

    Parent plan: PLAN-deleted-object-sweep.md.

    Planning effort: medium, as the master plan specifies. This phase changes no daemon code. It raises one configuration value on one cluster, watches what the database tier pays, adds the regression guard the mission asks for, and writes down how to do the same on another cluster. The judgement in it is the drain rate and the "clean enough to raise it again" test, and both are decided below rather than left to the implementer.

    Decisions continue the plan's single sequence: phase 3 used D14-D23, so this phase starts at D24.

    Scope¶

    In:

    • The 24 hour reading of phase 3 on sfcbr, which brief 3g asked this phase's planning commit to record (recorded in the master plan under "What phase 3 measured"; see the survey for why it is a 16 hour reading today and what completes it).
    • Raising DELETED_OBJECT_SWEEP_MAX_OBJECTS on sfcbr, in steps, with a written test for whether the tier absorbed each step (D24, D25).
    • A regression guard: a nightly fact in the 33fl infra report that turns the backlog gauge and the hard-delete counter into "days behind" per object type and flags a type that is behind or growing (D26).
    • The answer to phase 3's D23: whether the sweep's RPC pairs need load-budget entries, decided from the rate they settle at once the backlog is gone (D27).
    • The closing condition for #4473, the soak that proves it, and the #4399 reading the master plan calls a natural experiment (D28, D29).
    • Operator documentation of all of the above.

    Out:

    • Changing the shipped default of 100 objects a pass. It is nearly four times sfcbr's arrival rate and sfcbr is the busiest cluster we know of (D25).
    • A Grafana alert on the backlog. No Shaken Fist metric is alerted on today and the signal is day-scale (D26).
    • #3922 (errored objects are never hard deleted). It adds arrivals. It becomes safe to pick up once D28's soak has passed, and the master plan's Future work says so.
    • Anything about the sweep's code. One observation about it is recorded under "What the survey found" and left where it is.

    What the survey found¶

    All references are to develop at 7d013a5d2a and to 33fl at 2f77f500 unless stated. The master plan's phase 4 section was written before phases 2 and 3 ran, and the survey corrected four of its claims at the source (the master plan's phase 4 section now carries a Revised while planning phase 4 note, and the docs/plans/index.md row was rewritten); this section records what was found so no later step repeats the check.

    1. Phase 3 went live on sfcbr at 2026-10-28T09:09Z, when the nightly deploy restarted sf-cluster and sf-4 (pid 1577153) was elected. At planning time it had run for 16.7 hours, not the 24 that brief 3g asks for. The numbers under "What phase 3 measured" in the master plan are therefore a 16 hour window ending 2026-10-29T01:55Z, cross-checked against the per-pass log lines in Loki for the same period, and the first step of this phase re-runs them over the full first 24 hours (window ending 2026-10-29T09:10Z) and replaces them in the same pull request. The reading will not change in kind: every number below is a rate.

    2. The cap, not the time budget, sets sfcbr's drain rate. Of 913 passes since the deploy, 891 took exactly 100 entries (the cap) and 22 fewer, those being the passes that ran into the 20 second budget with a turn of per-object namespaces or a 3.4 second blob batch inside them. Mean processing time per pass is 4.6 seconds, median 3.2. The sweep ran 54.7 passes an hour rather than 60: the pass is registered every 30 seconds (phase 3's D19) but the maintenance loop also runs the hourly backlog count and the other jobs, and every pass that overran the budget pushed the next one out. That is about 9% of capacity and is not worth a change; D24's arithmetic uses 55 passes an hour.

    3. Throughput per type is the cap divided by the types with work. Thirteen types held a backlog and each was hard deleted about 8,000 times in 16 hours, within 2% of each other. The round robin takes one turn of 25 from each type with work, so at a cap of 100 a pass serves four types and the rotation completes every 3.25 passes. That is the fairness phase 2 asked for, and it is why raising the cap raises every type's share equally until a type drains, when its share passes to the rest. The total drain rate is what the cap sets: 86,075 hard deleted in 16 hours against about 43,000 arrivals a day, so the backlog fell by 61,074 in the 17 hours from 08:00Z to 01:00Z (1,655,145 to 1,594,071), about 86,000 a day. At that rate the 1.59M backlog takes about 18.5 days.

    4. The master plan said the throttle "defaults to about phase 2's rate". It defaults to about phase 2's intended rate (D18's 100 a pass), which phase 3's cadence fix turned into 1.7 times phase 2's measured rate: 129,000 a day at the cap against phase 2's 76,840. Corrected in the master plan's phase 4 section.

    5. The master plan said to add load-budget entries "once the drain rate is settled". At the rates measured, every one of the sweep's RPC pairs is already under the unbudgeted ceiling of max(0.25, 0.05 x nodes) = 0.30/s on sfcbr's six nodes (shakenfist/data/database_load_budget.yaml:110-111): HardDeleteObjects 0.088/s from sf-cluster and 0.023/s from sf-api, the three base deletes (DeleteObjectState, DeleteObjectEvents, DeleteObjectMetadata) 0.143/s each from the per-object types, DeleteNamespace 0.131/s, GetObjectsByState 0.26/s against its budgeted 0.222/s base (tolerance is twice that), and the hourly CountObjectsByState 0.008/s. The drain will push the per-object namespace pairs over the ceiling for its duration, and once the backlog is gone every pair falls to its arrival-driven rate, which is lower than today's. So the honest expectation is that no entry is needed. The question is answered from the settled rate rather than assumed (D27), and the master plan's phase 4 section now says so. Nothing on sfcbr enforces the ceiling during the drain: the generated rules in examples/prometheus-database-load-rules.yaml are not deployed on the 33fl Prometheus (phase 2's execution notes checked), the 33fl nightly ratchet warns about an unbaselined pair only above 2.0/s (claude_infra_reports/facts/2026-10-28.json, thresholds), and CI's test_no_unbudgeted_fixed_rate_database_polling sees CI's own cluster. That is acceptable for a bounded drain and is written down here so the ratchet's reader is not surprised by a namespace delete pair at 0.4/s for three days.

    6. How comparable signals are reported. The master plan offered "an entry in the 33fl infra report ... or an alert, whichever matches how comparable signals are already reported". The comparable signal is sf-database load, which is a nightly report ratchet (shakenfist_db_load, tools/infra-report-precompute.py:7012 in 33fl) and not an alert; config/maui/grafana/alerting/rules.yml in 33fl alerts on no Shaken Fist metric at all, and the report's own convention for a day-scale signal with no 3am action is a ratchet (compute_timer_overhead's note, 33fl :2643). So the guard is a report fact (D26). The operator guide's suggestion of an absent() alert on the gauge stays as operator advice.

    7. Where the knob is set on sfcbr. sfcbr's cluster configuration is extra_config in 33fl's group_vars/allsf.yml:47, applied with sf-ctl set-config by the collection's cluster configuration play and read back and asserted by sfcbr.yml:284-300 on every deploy. shakenfist/config.py:1260 loads cluster_config once at import, so a changed value reaches sf-cluster only on restart. The nightly deploy restarts the daemons when code, rendered config or units changed (roles/node/tasks/register.yml:33-38, :197-210), which on sfcbr is most nights because develop moves; a night it does not is detectable from the pass log (the total field stays at or under 100) and is fixed with a restart of sf-cluster. A hand sf-ctl set-config would also work but would not be in 33fl's history, and 33fl is where every other sfcbr setting lives.

    Decisions¶

    D24 -- Raise sfcbr's cap to 400 a pass, then 800, each held for a day¶

    The drain is set by DELETED_OBJECT_SWEEP_MAX_OBJECTS, applied through 33fl's extra_config (finding 7). The first step is 400, four times today's value:

    • Capacity is 400 x 55 passes x 24 = 528,000 a day; net of 43,000 arrivals, the 1.59M backlog drains in about 3.5 days.
    • A pass at 400 is sixteen turns of 25. Fifteen are batches at about 0.25 seconds (finding 3's latencies) and one is a turn of 25 namespaces at 0.25 seconds each, so a pass processes for about 10 seconds of its 20. The budget is not binding, and if the tier slows it becomes binding before anything else does, which is the self-limit phase 3's D18 kept.
    • The tier pays about 15 more commits a minute for the batches and about 125 for the namespace turn, so about 2.5 commits a second more than today, and about 5 requests a second more. Phase 2 paid 3.3 commits a second more than phase 3 does for less than half the deletes, and the tier absorbed it. This step is inside what has already been seen to work.

    After 24 hours at 400, if every clean test in D25 holds, the cap goes to 800 (about 1.0M a day; the remainder drains in a day and a half), and is left there until the backlog is gone, at which point the sweep is arrival-bound whatever the cap says. If a test fails the cap is held, or dropped back to 100, as D25 says.

    This is the decision most likely to be argued with, from either side. Flat out would be a cap of 1,000 or more with the time budget as the only limit: the master plan ruled that out as an unmeasured write load on labqnap, and the two-step version costs one extra day. The cautious alternative is to leave 100 and wait 18 days, which is a fortnight of elevated GetInstance costs for every lister on sfcbr and a fortnight before #4399 can be read.

    D25 -- What "the tier absorbed it" means, decided before the drain¶

    A step is clean when, over its first 24 hours and against the phase 3 baseline (the 16 hour window in "What phase 3 measured", refreshed by 4a):

    1. MariaDB commits rose by no more than 5 a second (sum(rate(mysql_global_status_commands_total{command="commit"}[24h])), baseline 14.8/s).
    2. Batch latency for the operation types stayed under 1.5 times the baseline (cluster_deleted_object_batch_seconds per type, baseline 0.21-0.25 s; so under 0.38 s), and the namespace per-object cost under 0.4 s (baseline 0.25).
    3. InnoDB row lock waits did not double (sum(rate(mysql_global_status_innodb_row_lock_waits[24h]))).
    4. No sf-database process restarted outside a deploy (changes(process_start_time_seconds{job="shakenfist-database"}[1d]) per instance), and Loki shows no watchdog SIGABRT of a gateway (the signature in the sf-database watchdog memory: {job="shakenfist"} |= "sf-database" |= "SIGABRT", tenant sfcbr).
    5. The sweep's own median processing time per pass stayed under 15 seconds (the elapsed_seconds field of the pass log line), so the budget was not what bounded the drain.
    6. The CI runner flake-rate alert did not fire for a cause that traces to sfcbr's database tier.

    Holding on a failed test means leaving the cap where it is for another day and re-reading. Dropping back means editing extra_config to 100 and, to take effect before the next nightly deploy, sf-ctl set-config DELETED_OBJECT_SWEEP_MAX_OBJECTS 100 on a database node followed by a restart of sf-cluster on every node. Both are recorded in the execution notes with the numbers that decided them.

    The shipped default stays at 100. It is four times sfcbr's arrivals, sfcbr is the busiest cluster we know of, and a cluster that has accumulated a backlog is better served by an operator reading the new "Draining a backlog" section than by a default sized for a one-off drain.

    D26 -- The regression guard is a nightly report fact, shakenfist_sweep_backlog¶

    Added to 33fl's tools/infra-report-precompute.py beside compute_sf_db_load, registered as a section, with a note, a prompt subsection under section 19, a docs/infra-report-facts.md bullet, FLAG_CHANGE_SPECS entries and unit tests. From four instant queries at the report time:

    max by (object_type) (cluster_deleted_object_backlog)
max by (object_type) (max_over_time(cluster_deleted_object_backlog[2h] offset 24h))
sum by (object_type) (increase(cluster_deleted_object_seconds_count{outcome="hard_deleted"}[24h]))
sum by (object_type) (increase(cluster_deleted_object_seconds_count{outcome="failed"}[24h]))

    it computes, per type: backlog, backlog_prior (null when the prior sample is missing), hard_deleted_24h, arrivals_24h = hard_deleted_24h + (backlog - backlog_prior) clamped at zero, days_behind = backlog / arrivals_24h (null when arrivals are under 100 a day, where the ratio means nothing), and two flags: behind when days_behind is over 1.0 and backlog is at least 1,000, and growing when backlog - backlog_prior is at least the larger of 1,000 and a tenth of backlog_prior. Cluster-wide it carries total_backlog, total_prior, hard_deleted_24h, failed_24h, net_per_day, and days_to_drain (total_backlog / -net_per_day when the net is negative and the total is over 50,000) so the report can say "draining, about N days left" during this phase and "behind" after it.

    Three things about the gauge that the function must respect, and the tests pin: it is leader-only, so a leadership change in the last hour leaves it absent, and absence is reported as unavailable for the whole section rather than read as zero; two leaders can briefly both publish it, so it is read with max by, never sum by; and the 24-hour-ago sample is read over a two hour window because the count is hourly and the series may have a gap.

    The mission's bound is 24 hours (master plan, mission 1), which is what days_behind > 1 encodes. The 1,000 floor keeps a type with forty objects inside its one hour grace period from flagging; an object type that matters has more arrivals than that in a day.

    Why not an alert: finding 6. Why not a streak counter like shakenfist_db_load's days_flagged: the closing condition (D28) reads seven consecutive nightly facts files, which is a management session's jq rather than code, and the report's "Resolved since the previous night" section already comes from FLAG_CHANGE_SPECS.

    Risks and mitigations¶

    • The drain is a write load labqnap has not carried. Phase 1 tied the per-commit cost to innodb_flush_log_at_trx_commit=1 on labqnap's storage, and the sf-database watchdog SIGABRTs of earlier this year were labqnap's buffer pool. Mitigation: D24 steps rather than jumps, D25 is written before the first step and names the queries, the time budget bounds a pass whatever the cap says, and the way back is one line in 33fl plus a restart. The management session checks, at 24 hours per step.
    • The cap change does not take effect. cluster_config is read at start, and the nightly deploy restarts sf-cluster only on change. Mitigation: brief 4d's first step confirms from the pass log before the clock starts.
    • The regression guard reads absence as zero. A leadership change drops the gauge for up to an hour. Mitigation: D26's unavailable branch and test (d); the prompt says unavailable means not checked.
    • The guard double-counts during a leadership handover. Mitigation: max by, and test (f) asserts on the expression.
    • 33fl's nightly report edits the same files. prompt.txt and the pre-compute are touched by the report workflow and by hand. Mitigation: 4b is small and lands the day it is opened; a conflict is a rebase.
    • The soak is interrupted by a deploy that resets the sweep. A restart loses the in-memory work lists but not the backlog or the counters, and the gauge returns within an hour. Mitigation: D28 counts nights, not hours, and an unavailable night is not a dirty night; it is a night that does not count.
    • #4399 reads as unchanged. That is a result (D29), not a failure of this phase.
    • The fact flags sfcbr's small types forever. A type with a few hundred objects inside their grace period and few arrivals would have a high days_behind. Mitigation: the 1,000 floor and the 100-a-day arrivals minimum, both chosen from the per-type numbers in finding 3; test (b)'s third type pins the floor.
